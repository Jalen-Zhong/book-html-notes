CHAPTER 1 · 2/3

Eight gauges often averaged into total power: education, competitiveness, innovation/tech, output, world-trade share, military, financial-center strength, reserve-currency status. They typically rise as a chain — education → innovation → trade and military → output → financial center → (lagged) reserve currency — and fade in a similar order. A reserve currency, like a lingua franca, often outlasts the empire’s peak by habit.¹

Strengths arrive in bundles; weaknesses do too.

The Rise

After a new order: relatively low debts, narrower wealth/values/political gaps, cooperative prosperity, decent education and infrastructure, capable leadership, and a peace policed by one or more dominant powers. Key moves: strong education (including character and work ethic), openness to best practices, civil-military-government coordination, rising competitiveness and trade share, military protection of routes, income funding R&D, and capital markets that incentivize builders — the Dutch VOC and stock exchange were machine parts, not trivia.²

Financial centers follow: Amsterdam → London → New York, with Shanghai rising fast. When trade clears in the currency and savers want to hold it, reserve status grants cheaper borrowing.

The Top

Rewards hide seeds of decline. Labor grows expensive; rivals copy methods; competitiveness erodes. Wealth brings leisure and refinement; generations slide from builders to inheritors. People lever good times into bubbles; wealth gaps self-reinforce as the rich shape politics and education — “haves” vs “have-nots.” Gaps may not explode while living standards still rise for most.³

Reserve-currency “exorbitant privilege” lets the empire borrow more — looking stronger near-term, weaker long-term. When the cost of empire exceeds its take, empire becomes a loss-maker. Rich countries borrowing from poorer, higher-saving ones is an early tell of power shift.

The Decline

Decline usually comes from internal economic weakness plus fighting, costly external fighting, or both — gradually, then suddenly. When debts are huge and borrowing cannot roll, printing usually beats hard default: devaluation and inflation follow. Fiscal stress plus gaps fuels left/right populism; capitalism and elites get blamed; taxes and capital flight reinforce each other; capital controls can spark panic.⁴

Productivity falls, the pie shrinks, strongmen promise order — democracy is most stressed here. Civil war/revolution forges a new internal order; only when that lines up with a capable external challenger can the world order itself flip. Rivals exploit weakness; military bills arrive when finances are worst. Without a world referee, disputes end as tests of power. When debt, civil conflict, foreign war, and lost faith in the currency align, a world-order change is typically at the door.

Building eras often run ~40–80 years; destruction/restructuring ~10–20 — with wide variance.
