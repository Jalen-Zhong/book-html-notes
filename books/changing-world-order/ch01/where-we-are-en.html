CHAPTER 1 · 3/3

The last major destroy-and-restructure run was roughly 1930–45. The postwar order opened with Bretton Woods and US-centered governance (UN, World Bank, IMF, etc.). The US then held about two-thirds of world gold, about half of world output, and a nuclear monopoly plus unmatched conventional forces.¹

~75 years on at the time of writing: major reserve empires sit late in the long-term debt cycle with conventional monetary policy impaired; fragmented governments fill holes with borrowed money while central banks print; wealth and values gaps are wide; a rising power competes in trade, tech, capital, and geopolitics — plus a pandemic. Simultaneously, human + machine intelligence is inventing unprecedented problem-solving. Handled well, a different prosperity can follow; he is equally sure radical change will traumatize many.²

A health dashboard paints gauges green→red; the average hints whether you are closer to building or breaking.

Dutch → British → US: a succession checklist

When reserve batons pass, the checklist often lights up together: debt restructuring/crisis; internal revolution (peaceful or violent) shifting wealth from haves to have-nots; external war; currency breakdown; then new domestic and world orders. Not calendar mysticism — cause and effect crowding into one storm.³

Ch.1 closes: beyond the productivity uptrend, cycles mint prosperous building and painful restructuring; strong readings bias the path ahead strong, weak readings weak. Details and cases come later.
