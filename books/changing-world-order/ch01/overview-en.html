CHAPTER 1 · 1/3

Chapter 1 crushes the Dutch, British, and American reserve empires — plus Germany, France, Russia, India, Japan, China, and Chinese dynasties back to the Tang — into a usable archetype. The point is a measuring stick for where we are in the cycle, not a museum tour.¹

He calls the research audacious, humbling, necessary, and fascinating: archives, scholars, practitioners, drafts, stress tests, redrafts until diminishing returns. He will not swear decimal precision; “mostly right” is enough to put once-in-a-lifetime, historically recurrent events on the radar.

An archetype is an average path; deviations are the doorway to “why this time differs.”

The timeless fight: make, take, distribute

Across time, the hardest plotline is making, taking, and distributing wealth and power; ideology and religion often intertwine. Those with wealth typically own the means of producing it and ally with those who hold political power to set and enforce rules. Class costumes change — landed nobles to capitalists and elected or authoritarian officials — but the cooperate-and-compete structure rhymes.²

The usual movie: a tiny share of people control outsized wealth and power → overreach → hard times hit the least powerful first → conflict becomes revolution/civil war → a new order → the cycle restarts.

Evolution, cycles, and bumps

Evolution is the only lasting force: adaptation and learning lift living standards; cycles swing around that path — peace/war, boom/bust, left/right, cohesion/fragmentation. Push past equilibrium and the opposite overshoots. Fear, greed, and jealousy barely change, so cycle shapes stay family-similar.³

Productivity is the main engine of rising wealth and longevity. The printing press, scientific revolution, Enlightenment, capitalism, and Industrial Revolution steepened the slope. Wealth shifted from land to machines and increasingly to data and compute — so fights shift toward who owns data and how it becomes power.
