CHAPTER 5 · 4/4

Stage 6 civil wars/revolutions: the dispute-resolution system flips from “still works” to “broken beyond repair” — violence escalates, leadership loses control. Historian-stamped civil-war periods usually last a few years; winners are symbolized by who occupies capital government buildings, yet consolidation fights often continue after the “official” end.¹

Early fighting can still look orderly; emotion and win-at-all-costs push brutality past what Stage 5 thought possible. Elites and moderates flee, are jailed, or killed. The Russian Revolution, Meiji Restoration, Spanish Civil War, Chinese Civil War, French Revolution span the spectrum: some install multi-decade domestic orders (e.g., Soviet 1917–91); others rebuild via opening and modernization (Meiji).

Rebuild speed hinges on how severe Stage 6 was and whether new leaders can do Stage 1–2 consolidation and engineering. Keep recognizing the 5→6 crossing (deaths, system failure) early — wealth and personal risk rise nonlinearly in hot conflict.²

Stage 6 is not a morality-play finale — it is an expensive doorway into the next Stage 1.
Section notes
¹ Official end ≠ consolidation end Capital capture is a narrative stamp; purges and faction fights can drag.
Why it matters：Avoid premature “new era is stable” calls.
Sources：Book Ch.5 Stage 6
