CHAPTER 5 · 4/4

Stage 6 civil wars/revolutions: the dispute-resolution system flips from “still works” to “broken beyond repair” — violence escalates, leadership loses control. Historian-stamped civil-war periods usually last a few years; winners are symbolized by who occupies capital government buildings, yet consolidation fights often continue after the “official” end.¹

Early fighting can still look orderly; emotion and win-at-all-costs push brutality past what Stage 5 thought possible. Elites and moderates flee, are jailed, or killed. Named hair-curling cases: Spanish Civil War, Chinese Civil War, Russian Revolution, French Revolution. Russia’s 1917 order lasted to 1991 (~74 years), failed reform attempts, then collapse and classic Stage 1–2 rebuild; Meiji Restoration followed a ~three-year revolution (1866–69) that opened Japan, overturned the four-class order, pushed universal elementary education and capitalism — then ran a classic Big Cycle until defeat ended the Meiji order ~1869–1945 (~76 years).²

Revolutionary leaders often come from educated middle-class backgrounds: Danton/Marat/Robespierre, Lenin/Trotsky, Mao/Zhou Enlai — typically idealistic intellectuals who evolve into win-at-all-costs brutal revolutionaries. Start/end dates are historian stamps: 1789-07-14 Bastille is often labeled the French Revolution’s start, yet almost no one then knew they were at the door or how brutal it would become.

Rebuild speed hinges on how severe Stage 6 was and whether new leaders can do Stage 1–2 consolidation and engineering. Keep recognizing the 5→6 crossing (deaths, system failure) early — wealth and personal risk rise nonlinearly in hot conflict; “when in doubt, get out” is often already too late once Stage 6 is stamped.

Stage 6 is not a morality-play finale — it is an expensive doorway into the next Stage 1.
