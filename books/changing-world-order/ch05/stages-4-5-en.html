CHAPTER 5 · 3/4

④ Excesses

Debt-financed goods, services, and assets outrun future cash flows → bubbles. People treat big-up assets as “good” not “expensive,” lever up, and spiral net worth and borrowing capacity until the break. Classic refs: Japan 1988–90, US 1929 and 2006–07, late-1970s LatAm commodity producers. Best leaders are “grounded disciplinarians” who restrain overheating; history more often shows post-wealth decadence (Nero / Louis XIV / Wanli-type waste).¹

⑤ Bad finances and intense conflict

After Stage 3’s easy debt and Stage 4 excess, class tension meets worsening finance; how conflict is handled decides peaceful change or violence. The book dwells here because many countries — especially the US at writing — are read as Stage 5.

Classic toxic mix: (1) entity in bad financial shape; (2) large income/wealth/values gaps; (3) severe negative economic shock. Large deficits with too few non-CB buyers → non-printers tax/cut (capital flight), printers buy their own debt (investors flee money/debt). Broad productivity bets on education, infrastructure, and high-return R&D are near-universal rise ingredients — and the constructive counter to the toxic mix.²

Stage 5 marker list (stack = danger)

  • Decadent spending shifts from Stage 4 “fashion” to Stage 5 “grotesque,” often still debt-funded.
  • Populism: anti-elite, emotional, left/right extremes squeeze moderates — moderates shrink in 5, vanish in 6.
  • Class war and scapegoating (ethnic/religious/“capitalist” labels).
  • Weaponized media / propaganda logic; trial-by-media.
  • Rules yield to win-at-all-costs; politicized law/police; paramilitary thugs.
  • Protests turn violent; deaths often stamp entry to Stage 6.
  • Principle: when in doubt, get out — late Stage 5 capital controls and exit limits tighten.

Democracy can “bend” via elections, but fragmented ineffectiveness invites strongmen; federal center–state fights are extra markers. Stage 5 wants a “strong peacemaker”; else a “strong fighter” leads through Stage 6.³

Stage 5’s core question: how far can the system bend before it breaks?
