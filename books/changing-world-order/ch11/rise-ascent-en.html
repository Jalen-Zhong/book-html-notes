CHAPTER 11 · 2/4

Founding to Gilded Age

US builds post-revolution order unusually peacefully: Articles → Constitution → institutions and staffing. After the Civil War, Second Industrial Revolution lifts incomes, tech, and wealth — plus huge gaps, Progressive trust-busting, and the 1913 income-tax amendment. New York becomes a global financial center only after being a trade hub; fragmented banking brings repeated panics; Fed arrives 1913. By ~1910 US equity market cap surpasses Britain’s.¹

World wars lift the dollar

In WWI the US enters late and keeps gold convertibility longer; Allied debts owed to the US boost dollar denomination. Versailles sets a new order; losers owe gold. Roaring ’20s debt/bubble, Fed tightening, Depression, fights within/between countries, hot war a decade later. WWII: US relative win — fighting mostly offshore, sales/lending profits, lower relative deaths — 1945 wealth/power shift.²

Dollar ascent was not straight: fragile banking history first, then wartime balance-sheet dividends.
