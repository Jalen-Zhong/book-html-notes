CHAPTER 11 · 4/4

Volcker tightness to debt-financed booms

1979 Volcker constrains money growth; rates “highest since Jesus Christ” crush inflation psychology; Reagan/Thatcher swing right and confront unions. Author wrongly forecasts depression from the debt crisis — lesson: when debts are in printable currency, crises can be managed. 1980s US boom with falling inflation; 1990–2008 globalization, digitalization, Soviet collapse, China rise, wider domestic gaps. 2008 rates hit 0 → MP1 to money-printing asset buys (MP2); 2020 pandemic adds fiscal–monetary coordination (MP3).¹

Now: polarization and watch markers

Party extremism up, cross-aisle compromise down; wealth/values gaps widen. The book cites public-survey magnitudes: e.g. Pew 2019 majorities seeing the other as more immoral; ~80% of Democrats think Republicans are taken over by racists and ~82% of Republicans think Democrats are taken over by socialists; parental displeasure at cross-party marriage rises from ~5% in 1960 to roughly a third–half of parents recently. Model: ~70% through Big Cycle, not Stage 6 hot war, but conflict high. Three escalation markers watched: rules disregarded, emotional mutual attack, blood spilled. Next chapter: rising China and US–China conflict surfaces.²

Printing and buying debt is now standard operating procedure — until it stops working.
