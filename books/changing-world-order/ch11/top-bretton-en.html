CHAPTER 11 · 3/4

Postwar geopolitics and Bretton Woods

US, USSR, UK emerge; US richest and strongest; Cold War follows — Truman Doctrine, NATO/Warsaw, nukes — Russia rises vs US until ~1980 yet never near-peers, then Russia declines, China ascends, US gradual decline. Bretton Woods (1944, ~44 countries): dollar to gold, others to dollar; Marshall/Dodge aid Europe/Japan and dollarize the world. Brief postwar recession: unemployment roughly doubles (to ~4%), ~20M leave military-adjacent jobs; then classic upswing — consumption, mortgages, exports, equities; US stocks peak ~1966, ending ~16 good years. Foreign branches: only 13 US banks in 1965, 79 by 1970, nearly every major bank by 1980 (~787 branches).¹

1970s: balance of payments and stagflation

Foreign CBs swap non-yielding gold for yielding dollar debt (1945–71) until claims dwarf gold and reserves fall — bank-run logic. Late-1960s: real growth near 0%, inflation ~6%, short rates ~8%, unemployment ~4%; decade stocks ~+8% annualized, vol-matched bonds ~−3%. Vietnam, guns-and-butter, Germany/Japan catch up; Aug 1971 Nixon ends gold convertibility → fiat decade: high inflation, oil/food shocks, two tightness/recession waves. Gold from official $35 toward ~$850 in 1980.²

Reserve privilege makes “we can always afford it” feel true — until the gold window is run-closed.
