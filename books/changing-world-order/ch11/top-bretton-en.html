CHAPTER 11 · 3/4

Postwar geopolitics and Bretton Woods

US, USSR, UK emerge; US richest and strongest; Cold War follows — Truman Doctrine, NATO/Warsaw, nukes. US remains strongest military overall but not 1945 monopoly; Taiwan scenarios worry. Bretton Woods: dollar to gold, others to dollar; Marshall/Dodge aid Europe/Japan and dollarize the world. After a brief postwar recession, classic upswing: consumption, mortgages, exports, equities, New York center.¹

1970s: balance of payments and stagflation

Foreign CBs swap non-yielding gold for yielding dollar debt until claims dwarf gold and reserves fall — bank-run logic. Vietnam, guns-and-butter, Germany/Japan catch up; Aug 1971 Nixon ends gold convertibility → fiat decade: high inflation, oil/food shocks, two tightness/recession waves. Gold from official $35 toward ~$850 in 1980.²

Reserve privilege makes “we can always afford it” feel true — until the gold window is run-closed.
