CHAPTER 4 · 3/3

Is gold the right yardstick? Also watch money versus goods and services (CPI baskets). Gold and inflation baskets do not always move together, yet the debt-crisis → print → devalue family resemblance remains: late-cycle buying power versus real things is easier to dilute.¹

Devaluation ≠ instant loss of reserve status

Cheapening a currency and losing reserve status are related but not identical; both often ride debt crises. Losing reserve status is slower and more political: holders reallocate systemically when economic and political primacy has already shifted to a rival and a credible alternative exists.²

Shared patterns (Part II empire cases expand them):

  • Central-bank reserves often fall net before the official devaluation (sometimes years ahead); convertibility may suspend first.
  • Runs and devaluations often stack with wartime spending or postwar debts.
  • CBs first lift short rates, then capitulate into printing when the real economy hurts too much.
  • Outcomes diverge: the hinge is remaining economic and military power — the Dutch guilder collapsed relatively fast; sterling took multiple devaluations to fully lose reserve status; the US saw abrupt 1933/1971 moves and more gradual post-2000 cheapening vs gold without (at writing) losing the reserve role.
Reserve status dies when “rival ready + primacy already lost,” not from one devaluation headline alone.
