CHAPTER 12 · 5/7

1949–1976: foundation and insulated build-out

After civil-war victory, power consolidates fast: transport/comms repaired, banks nationalized under the new PBOC, credit tightened to stabilize the currency; land redistribution and the “iron rice bowl” bring stability while dulling merit pay. Mao and Zhou Enlai build institutional, governance, and infrastructure foundations; China stays relatively isolated under a strict planned system.¹

Growth, volatility, succession crisis

By 1976 average growth is decent and inflation mostly mild, but FX reserves remain tiny and China is still poor; violent policy experiments and social upheavals punctuate the path. After Mao and Zhou die in 1976, reformers defeat Gang of Four hardliners; Deng’s camp wins in 1978 and opens phase two.²

Phase one’s job is to weld the country back together — efficiency and openness are phase two’s homework.
