CHAPTER 12 · 2/7

Four millennia in one page

Bronze-age foundations; Confucius (551–479 BCE) shapes family/hierarchy expectations; Qin unifies; Han sets governance templates; Tang is often seen as an imperial high point. Song is innovative and commercially dynamic yet crushed by outside powers; Yuan, Ming, Qing each run founder-strength → excess/rigidity → disaster/conflict → turnover. Except for the steep 1840–1950 fall, China long ranked among top powers; today it is second to the US and closing.¹

Typical dynasty cycle ≈ six internal-order stages

Major dynasties often last ~250±150 years and repeat: ① consolidate → ② build institutions/allocation → ③ peace and prosperity → ④ excess debt and gaps → ⑤ toxic conflict → ⑥ civil war/revolution reset. Accelerants: land concentration and tax-base erosion, coin/paper fiscal collapse, neglected public works plus famine, elite luxury, and isolation/anti-commerce norms that weaken competitiveness.²

The Comprehensive Mirror is not décor — contemporary leaders treat dynastic lessons as OS patches.
