CHAPTER 12 · 4/7

Money and fiscal stress: dynastic silent killers

Song, Yuan, and Ming often turned to paper when metal coin was scarce — especially in war and disaster; when elites sheltered the tax base, printing temptation rose and high/hyperinflation sped collapse. Neglected public works amplified flood/drought shocks — “underinvestment → disaster → food scarcity → uprising” co-occurs at many late-dynasty endings.¹

From 1800: Century of Humiliation

After early-Qing expansion and prosperity, European powers forced trade with military edge; opium, Opium Wars, Taiping and other internal shocks accelerated decline until Qing collapse in 1912. For today’s leaders, “lag and be beaten” and mastering tech/military/financial autonomy are long memories, not slogans.²

Money collapses rarely alone — they fall with tax bases, wars, climate, and legitimacy.
