CHAPTER 12 · 1/7

Chapter 12 focuses on China’s own Big Cycle: unlike Dutch/British/US arcs that rose then declined, China’s last ~200 years were a long decline then rapid rise. Seven powers bottomed around 1940–50; competitiveness and trade took off after ~1980 — right after Deng’s reform and opening. After 2008 debt rose vs incomes; from 2012 governance, tech, education, and military strengthened further while US friction rose.¹

The author argues honesty matters most when emotions run hot; he has visited since 1984 and triangulated with scholars and practitioners. This chapter first asks how Chinese people see the world and themselves; the next covers US–China conflict surfaces. The lens remains the 18 determinants and eight powers, read as much as possible through Chinese eyes.²

Read China by putting humiliation → revolutionary foundation → reform/opening → assertive rise into one machine.
