CHAPTER 14 · 2/5

Evolution: extrapolable mega-trends

Long charts of population, life expectancy, and real output per person show wars, depressions, and pandemics often dwarfed at century scale by invention and diffusion. From 1900–45 real wealth per capita barely rose (late-cycle transition); after the postwar order the uptrend averaged ~4%/yr despite feeling bumpy in real time. Looking ahead ~20 years the book sketches rough lifts on the order of ~25–30%, 45%, 70%, and ~15% (illustrative, not point forecasts); lifespan may rise another ~20–25%+ for reasons seen and unseen.¹

Cycles, bumps, quality indicators

Versus the mega-trend, depressions, wealth wipes, war deaths, and pandemics still ruin many lives — averages understate sufferers’ pain. Success formula: bet evolutionary productivity upside, but not so sized that one bump ends the game. Quality indicators help tell paradigm shifts from passing fads.²

Extrapolating 1750 would miss capitalism and democracy — keep eyes on paradigm cracks.
