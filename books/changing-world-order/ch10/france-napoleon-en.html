CHAPTER 10 · 3/5

Why not France?

18th-c. France shone in education, publishing, Enlightenment (Paris arts/science books roughly double 1720–80); John Law’s 1716 note-issuing national bank and Mississippi Company convert state debt to equity into a classic bubble — rate caps (no interest above ~5%) then freeze capital. Costly wars (Augsburg, Spanish Succession, Austrian Succession, Seven Years’, American Revolution) outran gains: during the American war both Franco-British national debts ~£220M, but French debt service ~£14M/yr vs Britain’s ~£7M; deficits ~2–3% of GDP and ~⅓ of annual tax. Privileged estates resist tax; Third Estate (~98% of population) forms its own assembly; bad harvests 1788–89 spike bread prices.¹

Napoleon and the Vienna order

1791 National Convention declares a republic; Jan 1793 Louis XVI executed; Reign of Terror kills an estimated ~20–30k. By 1795 France is broke and the assignat hyperinflates; 1796 presses are publicly destroyed. Napoleon, the military hero, centralizes, stabilizes, then overreaches into Russia and loses — British finance and navy are decisive. Congress of Vienna (1814–15) rebuilds balance of power; Britain seeks no vast new European territories, only no new hegemon; “splendid isolation” focuses on trade and colonies — Imperial Century.²

Bubble + war finance + tax privilege = revolution script; Britain wins the hot war with a stronger balance sheet.
