CHAPTER 10 · 5/5

Pound’s long devaluation

After 1945 Britain carries huge debts and a costly empire, lagging competitiveness, and deep wealth/political gaps. Reserve currencies, like English, are hard to replace: 1950s central banks still hold lots of sterling; ~⅓ of trade still invoiced in pounds in 1960. 1947 convertibility trial sparks a run; 1949 ~30% dollar devaluation; 1967 again — DM becomes #2 reserve. Sterling Area and dollar-value guarantees cannot fix fundamentals.¹

Europe: coal-steel to euro to secondary power

Postwar order centers on the US: NATO, Bretton Woods, IMF/World Bank, New York finance. Schuman and Adenauer push a coal-steel community to make war “materially impossible,” then Rome, Schengen, Maastricht → EU and euro. Early 21st-c. Europe: high debt, weak growth, internal conflict/populism, relatively weak military — slipping from recent leading-empire status to secondary power.²

Next chapter turns the lens to the US and the dollar — and rising China.
