CHAPTER 10 · 2/5

Civil war, Glorious Revolution, Enlightenment base

Thirty Years’ War and Westphalia shatter Europe’s old order; England’s Civil War and Glorious Revolution weaken monarchy and strengthen Parliament — PMs need parliamentary confidence; merchant-family statesmen can shape the state. Enlightenment prizes reason, science, and power from the people; education and print widen the public sphere. Centralized fiscal capacity nearly doubles France’s tax burden; 1694 Bank of England liquidifies gilts and yields fall relatively.¹

Industrial Revolution

Education + inventiveness + capital + coal/iron ignite the First Industrial Revolution: Bacon (1561–1626)–style scientific thought as base; the book’s timeline strings 1712 steam engine, 1733 flying shuttle, 1764 spinning jenny, 1769 water frame/steam upgrade, 1785 power loom, 1801–25 locomotives and Manchester–Liverpool rail. Farm gains → urbanization → factories; ~1750 economic/financial overtake of the Dutch (decisive naval win ~30 years later); ~1800 living standards catch up; mid-19th c. ~20% of world output. Navy secures colonies and routes; London and the pound inherit the Dutch mantle; China trade shifts toward tea — seeding the Opium Wars.²

Britain’s rise looks obvious in retrospect; contemporaneous indicators still showed Dutch at peak and Bourbon France rising — clarity was never crystal.
