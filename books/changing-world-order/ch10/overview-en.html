CHAPTER 10 · 1/5

Chapter 10: world orders switch when near-peer powers (or coalitions) fight and winners set rules. Britain’s rise predates preeminence: build education, institutions, tech; challenge/defeat the Dutch; Napoleon is the last great rival. After Vienna, ~100 years of relative peace — at peak ~2.5% of world population produced >20% of income and controlled >20% of land and ~25% of people.¹

Eight powers: competitiveness, education, innovation/tech surge in the 1600s through 1800; output, trade share, military expand 1700–1900; finance center and pound overtake the guilder with greater lag.²

Same script: Dutch → British → American — different tech clothes and language.
Section notes
¹ Long runway before preeminence Rise stories start long before a country is clearly #1.
Why it matters：Avoid reading “sudden hegemony” as unearned.
Sources：Book Ch.10 opening
² Lag structure Education/tech → output/trade/military → finance → reserve currency.
Why it matters：Universal order for reading any empire arc.
Sources：Book Ch.10; Ch.1 eight powers
