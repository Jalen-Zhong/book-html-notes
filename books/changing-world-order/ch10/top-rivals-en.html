CHAPTER 10 · 4/5

Top: pound, empire, imbalances

~1850 UK export share peaks near 40% of global; 1850–1914 ~60% of trade invoiced in sterling. Later current-account surpluses lean on overseas investment income over trade deficits; Rothschild-era sovereign lending waves; Bank of England as lender of last resort. Seeds already planted: competitiveness slips vs Germany/US (Second Industrial Revolution), extreme inequality (top 1% >70% of wealth), rising rivals.¹

Rivals, great wars, two postwars

Unified Germany races in education/industry; Wilhelm II is more aggressive; naval arms race plus many European hatreds. 1914 ignites — WWI slaughter; Versailles carve-ups and reparations seed WWII. 1920s boom, 1929 bust, Depression fuel populism; some democracies redistribute, weaker states go dictatorial-expansionist. WWII costlier still; US/UK/USSR “win” while UK/USSR are economically damaged — US relative triumph.²

Peak pound networks and colonial income masked relative declines in output per worker.
