CHAPTER 9 · 2/3

Classic rise steps

1581–~1625: William the Silent leads the Eighty Years’ War revolt; provincial sovereignty creates checks (not a single monarch); culture prizes education, saving, merit, tolerance. Dutch invent ocean ships and capitalist finance, create VOC (~⅓ of world trade), fund navy to hold trade, and in 1609 Bank of Amsterdam cleans ~800 debased coins — bank guilders become trusted Type-2 money and a reserve.¹

Income/capita more than doubles most European peers; literacy ~2× world average; Amsterdam becomes the financial center — with only 1–2M people. At peak, Dutch account for ~¼ of major global inventions. Baltic and Russian trade priced and settled almost solely in guilders and Bank of Amsterdam bills.

Capital markets and Westphalia

1602 exchange lists VOC: first transnational joint-stock firm with clear transfer rules; more than 1 in 50 Dutch adults owned shares. Banking makes settlement lean on guilders and bills — later peak annual payments through the bank ~1.5× Dutch GDP (vs UK 1868 ~3.6; US 1955 ~2.7).

Thirty Years’ War (1618–48) ends in balance-of-power and war exhaustion: Westphalia invents sovereign borders, weakens Habsburgs/clergy; ~one-quarter of Europe’s population dies; French/Spanish finances hurt more than Dutch — Golden Age rides peace plus navy–shipping combo.²

Public equity channels savings into enterprise — great inventions often carry deadly side effects: bubbles and debt.
Section notes
¹ Bank guilder and ~800 coins Ledger money backed by hard currency cleaning chaotic coinage; Baltic settlement is mechanism evidence.
Why it matters：Mechanism of the first true global reserve currency.
Sources：Bank of Amsterdam histories; Quinn & Roberds
² Westphalia dividend and war exhaustion Sovereign-state system shields Dutch from Habsburg absorption; ~¼ population loss explains the long peace.
Why it matters：Internal checks + external balance = ascent window.
Sources：Thirty Years’ War casualty estimates; treaty narratives
