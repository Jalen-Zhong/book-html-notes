CHAPTER 9 · 2/3

Classic rise steps

1581–~1625: William the Silent leads revolt; provincial sovereignty creates checks; culture prizes education, saving, merit, tolerance. Dutch invent ocean ships and capitalist finance, create VOC (~⅓ of world trade), fund navy to hold trade, and in 1609 Bank of Amsterdam cleans chaotic coinage — bank guilders become trusted Type-2 money and a reserve.¹

Income/capita more than doubles most European peers; literacy ~2× world average; Amsterdam becomes the financial center — with only 1–2M people. At peak, Dutch account for ~¼ of major global inventions.

Capital markets and Westphalia

1602 exchange lists VOC: first transnational joint-stock firm with clear transfer rules and wide ownership. Banking makes settlement lean on guilders and bills. Thirty Years’ War ends in balance-of-power and war exhaustion: Westphalia invents sovereign borders, weakens Habsburgs/clergy; Dutch finances hurt less than France/Spain — Golden Age rides peace plus navy–shipping combo.²

Public equity channels savings into enterprise — great inventions often carry deadly side effects: bubbles and debt.
Section notes
