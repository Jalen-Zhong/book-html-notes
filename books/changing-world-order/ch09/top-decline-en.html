CHAPTER 9 · 3/3

Top: good life, lost edge, Anglo-Dutch rivalry

After the Golden Age, attention shifts to living well; education/tech edge erodes; VOC slips; Industrial Revolution elevates Britain. A vast empire costs too much for a small nation; naval wars (four Anglo-Dutch wars, etc.) over-indebt the Dutch. 1688 Glorious Revolution helps short-run (William III on England’s throne) but second-order effects move merchants/capital to London and re-fragment Dutch provinces.¹

Decline: run and reserve handover

From ~1750 Britain and France are stronger; Dutch more indebted, internally conflicted, militarily weak. As foreign earnings fall, rich savers buy British assets — yet guilder lags via trade inertia. Fourth Anglo-Dutch War (1780–84) wrecks navy and routes; VOC borrows heavily from Bank of Amsterdam; printing paper claims → inadequate metal → bank run → guilder loses store-of-wealth credibility; pound takes over. 1795 French client state; VOC nationalized, equity wiped.²

Reserve status lags other power gauges — until war and a run flip it overnight.
