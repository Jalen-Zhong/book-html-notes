CHAPTER 2 ADDENDUM · A

The addendum zooms pieces that would clog Chapter 2. First: which “self” is self-interest about — individual, family, tribe/community, state, country, empire, humankind, all life, the universe? The unit people will die for is usually the real power unit in conflict.¹

History moves the primary unit: before ~1650, tribes/states often outweighed modern countries; over ~150 years Europe saw state→nation→supranational coalescing — and Soviet-style breakups. Recently globalism ebbed and nationalism rose; in the US, diverging views of coexistence push people toward aligned states, raising states’ relative weight. Locus-of-control shifts rewrite rights and duties.

Are you watching coalescing or dissolving — and from which level to which?

Wealth ≠ money; wealth ≈ power

Wealth ≈ buying power, distinct from nominal money and credit — when money floods, numerical “wealth” can rise while real purchasing power falls. Real wealth is stuff you want to use; financial wealth is claims on future income or saleable paper with no intrinsic use. Over time, continuous production is wealth; expropriation without production spends down fast. Wealth and power co-produce: enough wealth buys influence; enough power reassigns wealth. Ruling orders are often bargains between them — until overthrown.²

The blunt lesson: entities that spend beyond earnings head toward turbulence; societies with more self-sufficient people tend to be stabler. The East India Company’s capital + commerce + force opening Mughal trade is a hard case of wealth-as-power.
