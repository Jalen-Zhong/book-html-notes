CHAPTER 2 · 1/3

Chapter 2 unpacks the big story into monitors. History is a perpetual-motion machine: causes and effects swap costumes. His workflow mirrors a chess player with a computer partner: observe → write principles → back-test → encode → refine → repeat. Humans invent laterally; machines spot patterns faster with less emotion.¹

The sample is knowingly incomplete: ~11 leading empires over 500 years, ~20 major countries over 100, major Chinese dynasties over ~1,400; Rome, Greece, Egypt, Byzantium, Mongols, Han, Sui, Arab, and Persian empires only glanced; Africa, South Asia, Pacific, and precolonial Americas not systematically covered. Unknowns dwarf knowns — yet a crude evolving model can still beat flying blind.

Determinants are both what exists and the energy that changes what exists.

Internal, external, and world orders

Families, firms, cities, states, and the international system all have orders for who holds power and how pies are split. Internal and external/world orders tug on each other, rewritten by human nature, culture, and circumstance. Most people watch existing institutions; he watches the timeless forces that rewrite them. A perfect model would nearly predestine the future; perfection is impossible, so the goal is a leg up.²

Quant + qual: measure what you can. Causal snapshots in the book: heavy debt + tight money → debt crisis. The three big cycles turning ugly together (debt + printing, internal gaps, rising rivals) → high risk of incumbent decline. Not an abstract list — Dutch/British/US handoffs and Chinese dynastic turns light the same dashboard red.

Remembering 3 / 5 / 8 / 18

Nested memory: the 3 big cycles matter most; add culture/character and inventiveness for a working 5; add geography, geology, and acts of nature for an everyday 8; the full list is about 18, from geography to class struggle and leadership.³

Nature set: geography, geology, disasters, genealogy (genetics as a minor slice). Human-nature set: which “self,” drive for wealth/power, capital markets, learning from history, multigenerational psychology, short-term bias, inventiveness, culture, openness, leadership, class conflict, and more. Not ornaments — switches on a causal chain. The addendum expands self-interest units, wealth definitions, five psychological stages, inventiveness, and class struggle with Magna Carta, French Revolution, and East India Company cases.

Next addendum leaf: how the self-unit migrates from tribes to the EU; why wealth ≠ money.
