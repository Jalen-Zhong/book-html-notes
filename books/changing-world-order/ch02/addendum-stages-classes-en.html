CHAPTER 2 ADDENDUM · B

The multigenerational psychological cycle pairs being poor/rich with feeling poor/rich into five stages — a behavioral read on countries and leaders.¹

  1. Poor and feel poor: low incomes, cash-precious, little credit; culture and capability sort who exits “early emerging.”
  2. Rich but feel poor: still hardworking exporters, high savers, efficient real investment plus reserve assets, heavy education/infrastructure — the most productive stage (“late emerging”).
  3. Rich and feel rich: high wages but prior capex still pays; priorities shift from precautionary saving to savoring; arts/sciences flourish; large countries become economic-military powers (“peak health”).
  4. Poorer yet feel rich: debts rise vs income; lifestyle inertia > competitive reality; military spend and twin deficits common; late bubbles (“early declining”).
  5. Poor and feel poor: bust and deleveraging; private shrink, public expand, CB printing; weak currency, falling global weight (“clearly declined”). Return to old peaks is slow — if it happens.
Stage tags are behavior predictors, not moral scores.

Inventiveness and class struggle

Human problem-solving has outrun problems in aggregate; net knowledge makes the path drift up, with Renaissance-like spurts on Big Cycle upswings. Working impression: innovation + commercial spirit + living capital markets → productivity jumps → wealth and power.²

Almost all recorded societies saw a tiny share control most wealth and power; elite alliances defend the order, fight each other, and clash with non-elites. Good times soften struggle; bad times — unresolvable debt, awful economies, or disasters — push toward revolution/civil war. Aristotle’s sting still lands: rich and poor quarrel, and victors often treat supremacy as loot rather than just government.
