HOW TO READ

Dalio opens by admitting he wrestled with completeness versus brevity. The compromise: bold the spine — skim bold for a quick-read edition; dive into regular weight for mechanisms and historical cases.¹ Bold is an official skim lane, not marketing fluff.

A second visual layer marks timeless principles with a red dot and italics — reusable decision notes across eras. Pull them into your own checklist without waiting for a full chapter’s proof.

Bold for speed; full text for depth; red-dot lines as sticky-note principles.

Addenda, glossary, and economicprinciples.org

Some topics — deeper walks of a determinant, for example — live as chapter addenda: optional side rooms so the main corridor stays clear. A back-of-book glossary explains chart abbreviations that would otherwise look like alphabet soup.

To keep the print volume sane, reference material, citations, and more index data sit at economicprinciples.org. The book is the map; the site is the archive. Public studies the Introduction names — Productivity and Structural Reform, Populism, Two Economies — are mostly free there too.²
