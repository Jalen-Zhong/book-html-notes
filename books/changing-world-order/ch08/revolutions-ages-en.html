CHAPTER 8 · 3/3

Revolutions and “ages”

What remade history after 1500 were shifts in thinking and rules for sharing wealth and power. Commercial Revolution (Italian city-states, florin, Venetian perpetuals) → Renaissance (reason/evidence, print, Medici-style patronage) → Exploration/colonialism (Iberian “sun never sets,” silver and the Price Revolution; after Zheng He, Ming/Japan turned inward) → Reformation and the Thirty Years’ War → Westphalia (1648) inventing modern borders and sovereignty.¹

Capitalism, science, industry, revolutionary waves

The Dutch invent public equity and the Amsterdam exchange (1602); Bank of Amsterdam supports the guilder as the first non-metal global reserve currency. Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment extend method into politics. First Industrial Revolution lets Britain overtake the Dutch. After Napoleon, Vienna opens a British-led century of relative peace. Western gunboats trigger Meiji success and China’s Century of Humiliation. Second Industrial Revolution and the Gilded Age lift the US. Communism reacts to capitalist gaps and fuels 20th-century revolutions.²

Watch both lines: centuries of evolving thought, and peace/prosperity ↔ depression/war order resets. Next: the Dutch and the guilder.
