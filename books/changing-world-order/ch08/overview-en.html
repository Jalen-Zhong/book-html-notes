CHAPTER 8 · 1/3

Chapter 8: Part I described how the perpetual-motion machine works; Part II shows what it produced over 500 years. This chapter is a tiny nutshell that stages the Dutch, British, American, and Chinese cases; Part III later squints at leading countries today. The start is not an abstract frame — go back to 1500, when the operating logic already matched today’s, but the scenery did not.¹

In 1500 the world felt “bigger”: ~25 miles/day on horseback; Paris–Rome often slower than Apollo’s Moon round-trip. Europe, Russia, and China were nearly separate worlds; borders unlike today’s meant near-constant neighbor fights. Meanwhile the Age of Exploration—led by Portugal and Spain—was already shrinking distances: ships that could travel the globe to trade and take wealth, with rulers taking a cut; Spanish silver coinage circulated as far as China, approaching near–reserve-currency status.²

Evolutionary uptrends advance; Big Cycles swing and bump around them — for five centuries.
