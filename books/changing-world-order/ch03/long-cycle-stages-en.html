CHAPTER 3 · 3/3

The long-term debt cycle’s archetypal six stages (not metronomic, but family-similar):

  1. Hard-money start: debts cleaned by restructuring/monetization; trust thin; money returns to gold/silver-type anchors.
  2. Claims on hard money: notes promise convertibility; banks (and temples, etc.) vault metal and issue claims.
  3. Debt expansion: claims are lent; banks lend onward at higher rates; assets and output boom; claims soar vs hard money and goods.
  4. Crisis, default, devaluation: income cannot service debt, or claims cannot convert as expected → a run; defending the hard link means deflationary depression, so links break, printing and devaluation follow — a “beautiful deleveraging” prints just enough.¹
  5. Fiat and debasement: pure fiat frees printing from metal stocks; governments often become top borrowers; debts get monetized. Four levers: austerity, default/restructuring, redistribution/taxes, print-and-devalue — printing is usually the most political path of least resistance.²
  6. Flight back to hard assets: overprinting sparks selling of debt and money into gold, silver, inflation-hardy equities, foreign assets, even rocks; governments impose controls. Then often a re-link to hard money or a foreign currency to rebuild trust.
Holding debt is like carrying a ticking bomb that pays you while it ticks and explodes when it stops — roughly every 50–75 years.

Three monetary systems

Across millennia: (1) hard money; (2) paper claims; (3) fiat. Need more money/credit and systems move 1→2→3; overprinting drives a flight back to hard assets and foreign currencies. The biblical Jubilee every 50 years institutionalizes debt forgiveness — calendarizing the cycle.³

Inside each regime there are usually 2–4 big debt crises — bank crises, write-downs, or 30%+ devaluations — that need not smash the whole currency system. The next chapter digs into why money’s value changes and the risks that follow.
