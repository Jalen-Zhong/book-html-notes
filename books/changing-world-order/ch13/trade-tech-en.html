CHAPTER 13 · 2/5

Trade/economic war

So far mostly classic tariffs and import limits (echoing Smoot-Hawley 1930) plus a limited “phase one” deal. US critiques cluster on market-access limits, industrial policy/tech extraction, and IP issues; both sides mobilize with “we are good, they cheat.” The more dangerous escalation is cutting essential imports (energy, critical tech, rare earths) — the pre-WWII US–Japan path is the author’s geographic/issue analogue.¹

Technology war

Tech war is deadlier than trade war: winners of tech likely win military and the rest. Both dominate big tech; China localizes fast yet still depends on foreign critical tech; the US fears competition and can weaponize cutoff. Decoupling and hedging external shrinkage with domestic markets is explicit — especially for China.²

Polite tariffs are foreplay; cutting oxygen (critical inputs) is the regime change.
