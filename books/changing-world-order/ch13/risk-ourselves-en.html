CHAPTER 13 · 5/5

Risk of unnecessary war

The author’s deepest fear is not “rationally calculated war” but unnecessary war born of honor, miscalculation, domestic political binding, and emotion spirals. History repeatedly shows conflicts neither side wanted yet slid into via commitments and face. Clear red-line talk, keeping off-ramps, and refusing to moralize every dispute into zero-sum are practical probability reducers.¹

War with ourselves: the enemy is us

For the US (and others), competitiveness is often decided internally: education, debt, infrastructure, polarization, rule of law, and whether productivity evolution continues. External rivals are mirrors; if internal order turns toxic, external contests get harder to win. The final chapter turns “how to see the future” into gauges and actions.²

The most expensive war is often the one that did not have to be fought.
