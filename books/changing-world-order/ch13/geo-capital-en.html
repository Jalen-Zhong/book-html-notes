CHAPTER 13 · 3/5

Geopolitical war

Issues include Taiwan, South China Sea, Hong Kong, human-rights narratives, alliance systems, and voice in international organizations. US postwar base networks are costly to maintain; for China, unification and periphery security are core-interest narratives. Knowing each other’s red lines/tripwires beats emotional mutual attack for avoiding runaway escalation.¹

Capital war

Capital war is about whose markets, payments, reserve currency, and sanctions tools dominate. Dollar dominance in reserves/payments makes financial sanctions asymmetrically sharp; China pushes RMB internationalization, bilateral local-currency settlement, and reserve diversification while still holding large USD assets — fragile “financial mutual assured destruction” symbiosis. CB reserve shares, capital controls, and investment bans are the dashboard.²

Sanctions power comes from network effects; dedollarization drive comes from politicizing that same network.
