CHAPTER 6 · 3/3

Wartime economic policy and controls

After Pearl Harbor, most belligerents activated classic wartime policies: rationing, production controls, price/wage controls, import/export limits, central banks pulled into war finance, allied resource coordination. Where markets stayed open, prices tracked battle odds and control intensity.¹

Regulatory toolkit hitting assets

Common mix: market closures, asset price controls, ownership limits, FX controls, soaring top tax rates, limits on new issuance and corporate profits. Closures trapped investors; losing a war often means near-total wipeout of wealth and power — so “safe assets” are not safe in hot war, mobility falls, and protecting wealth is extremely hard.

Close: cycles are not destiny

Every power has its time in the sun; in rich stages, staying productive, earning more than spending, making the system work for most, and avoiding excess debt and conflict can rewrite the path. Part II turns to the US, prior reserve empires, and possible successors. Principles remain: negotiate win–win lists, have power without abusing it, refuse agitation into stupid wars.²

First investing lesson in hot war: survival and liquidity beat “buy the dip” stories.
