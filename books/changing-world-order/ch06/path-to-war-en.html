CHAPTER 6 · 2/3

Case: path to WWII. After the 1929 Depression, countries fought internally over wealth → turned to more nationalist, autocratic, militarized leadership; the League could not constrain. Germany: the Young Plan had eased reparations and foreign troops left by ~1930, then depression drove unemployment near 25%; Hitler mobilized Versailles humiliation, won industrialist support against communists, became chancellor in 1933, used Weimar Article 48 and the Enabling Act to seize power, refused further reparations, left the League, and by 1934 was supreme leader.¹

Fascist-style rebuild plus big fiscal-monetary stimulus: privatize yet control, force banks to buy government bonds, monetize via the Reichsbank; Volkswagen and the Autobahn targeted Aryan living standards. Book magnitudes: unemployment ~25% when Hitler took power, near nil by 1938; per-capita income up ~22% in five years; real growth averaged over ~8%/yr 1934–38; German equities rose nearly 70% 1933–38 until the hot-war edge. Japan: resource scarcity + military expansion in China. US/UK began with appeasement and domestic priority; economic tools first.

Economic–geopolitical steps toward hot war

Classic economic-war tactics include tariffs/quotas, FX and capital controls, asset freezes, cutting clearing, embargoes/blockades (e.g., US oil embargo on Japan and Panama Canal access limits; France’s blockade of the UK in the Napoleonic Wars). The Spanish Civil War became a left/right populist proxy fight; Franco, with Hitler’s support, purged the left.²

1937 Hitler secret meeting on expansion, then Anschluss and seizure of oil-bearing Czech land; rapid conquests of Denmark, Norway, Low Countries, France; Axis alignment with Italy and Japan; June 1941 invasion of the USSR opened two fronts. Pacific: Japan’s Shanghai/Nanjing occupation — Nanjing alone ~200,000 civilians and disarmed combatants by the book’s estimate; the US stayed isolationist yet already supplied planes/pilots to Chiang. Roosevelt won re-election promising to stay out while acting economically; 1940 Export Control Act and Stimson sanctions; Pacific Fleet to Hawaii; 1941 Lend-Lease; US freezes of Japanese assets and iron/steel embargoes — vacuums left by weak colonial empires in Asia filled by Japan, colliding with US Pacific aims. Dec 7–8 1941 Pearl Harbor and Philippines attacks linked Pacific and European theaters into a decisive decade.

Unknowns dwarf knowns: rivals fight when powers are comparable — so both sides enter thinking they can win.
