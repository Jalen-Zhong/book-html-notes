CHAPTER 7 · 2/3

Big Cycle of capitalism and markets

Until ~1350, Christianity and Islam banned interest lending (and Judaism within the community) as usury. After rule changes in places like Italy, deposits, bonds, and stocks created “financial wealth” — wealth no longer only tangible. Paper money and credit expand elastically vs gold/silver: near-term stimulating, later depressing when repaid; in extremes bankers and capitalists are hanged figuratively or literally as fiat floods and wealth zeros.¹

Financial wealth today dwarfs real wealth: if holders tried to convert together, “promise density” would show. Paper and ledger promises matter only for what they buy.

Complete investor picture

Risk = failing to earn what spending needs — not standard deviation. Three big risks: portfolio can’t meet needs; average OK but path bankrupt; buying-power/confiscation/controls destroy nominal gains. Imagine investing across the 10 great powers each decade from 1900: seven saw near-wipeouts at least once; others still had awful decades. Skip pre-1945 and you miss destruction samples.²

Annualized table figures in “lost decades” must be multiplied by time — pain exceeds the headline rate.
