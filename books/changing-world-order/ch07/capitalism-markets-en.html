CHAPTER 7 · 2/3

Big Cycle of capitalism and markets

Until ~1350, Christianity and Islam banned interest lending (and Judaism within the community) as usury — borrowers over-borrowed and violence followed — so currency stayed “hard” (gold and silver). In the Age of Exploration, explorers and backers split hard-asset profits as the main path to fortunes. After rule changes in places like Italy, deposits, bonds, and stocks created “financial wealth” — wealth no longer only tangible; Florence’s Medici and peers turned ledger promises into expandable buying power. If banks can create credit ~5× base money, you need less gold and silver.¹

Financial wealth today dwarfs real wealth: if holders tried to convert together, “promise density” would show — like a bank run, but bonds and stocks are too large versus what they can buy; fiat CBs can print to meet demand, yet paper and ledger promises matter only for what they buy. Credit creation is near-term stimulating and later depressing when repaid; when promises exceed delivery, bankers and capitalists are hanged figuratively or literally as fiat floods and wealth zeros.

Complete investor picture

Risk = failing to earn what spending needs — not standard deviation. Three big risks: portfolio can’t meet needs; average OK but path bankrupt; buying-power/confiscation/controls destroy nominal gains. Imagine investing across the 10 greatest powers each decade from 1900 (skipping thinner markets more prone to bad outcomes): seven saw near-wipeouts at least once; others still had awful decades; Germany and Japan — easy “winner” bets at times — lost virtually all wealth and many lives in the World Wars; the US and UK (and a few others) were uniquely successful cases that still suffered great wealth destruction.²

Public decade-window annualized real-return tables remind: for a whole decade, losses are roughly ~8× the annualized figure and gains ~15× — “lost decade” pain exceeds the headline rate. Skip pre-1945 and you miss destruction samples; skip ~500 years globally and you pretend “this time won’t rhyme.”

Annualized table figures in “lost decades” must be multiplied by time — pain exceeds the headline rate.
