CHAPTER 7 · 3/3

Wealth confiscation and capital controls

Beyond awful returns sit confiscatory taxes, capital controls, and market closures. Long peace before WWI did not mean the next great war was obvious — conditions worsened 1900–14, then destructive decades arrived. Many countries show identifiable 20th-century confiscation and tight/rising capital-control windows; wartime death rates (internal and external) and tax drags on rolling long-horizon equity real returns are what the public tables are meant to convey: politics and tax are first-order return terms.¹

Chapter 5’s principle echoes here: when in doubt, get out — late Stage 5 exit and capital controls close doors; in hot war, “safe asset” stories fail under closures and ownership limits. German and Japanese wipeouts of wealth and lives in the World Wars are the hard lesson that developed markets can still go to zero.

Reviewing the big debt/capital-markets cycle

Upwave: debt and financial wealth/obligations expand; financial assets rise vs real assets. In money-devaluation phases, hard money and hard assets rise vs cash. Investors must keep asking: does interest compensate debasement and risk? (Hook Ch.4: fiat-era bills’ real-return center ~−0.1%, gold ~+1.6%.) Falling rates lift equity/bond marks; late-cycle repricing of discount rates and risk premiums can rewrite a decade overnight.²

Purpose of investing: a store of wealth convertible into buying power later. The Big Cycle warns the store itself can be diluted by fiat, locked by controls, or zeroed by war — diversification, liquidity, and humility about rule changes are principle-layer answers, not a slogan for one tape. Part I closes here: money, internal, external, and investor lenses are in place; Part II turns to empire cases.
