CHAPTER 7 · 3/3

Wealth confiscation and capital controls

Beyond awful returns sit confiscatory taxes, capital controls, and market closures. Long peace before WWI did not mean the next great war was obvious — conditions worsened 1900–14, then destructive decades arrived. Many countries show identifiable 20th-century confiscation and tight/rising capital-control windows; wartime death rates (internal and external) and tax drags on rolling long-horizon equity real returns are what the public tables are meant to convey: politics and tax are first-order return terms.¹

Reviewing the big debt/capital-markets cycle

Upwave: debt and financial wealth/obligations expand; financial assets rise vs real assets. In money-devaluation phases, hard money and hard assets rise vs cash. Investors must keep asking: does interest compensate debasement and risk? Falling rates lift equity/bond marks; late-cycle repricing of discount rates and risk premiums can rewrite a decade overnight.

Purpose of investing: a store of wealth convertible into buying power later. The Big Cycle warns the store itself can be diluted by fiat, locked by controls, or zeroed by war — diversification, liquidity, and humility about rule changes are principle-layer answers, not a slogan for one tape.²

Part I close: money, internal, external, and investor lenses are in place; Part II turns to empire cases.
