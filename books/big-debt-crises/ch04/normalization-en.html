⑤ Normalization: Making the Currency Holdable Again

Normalization arrives when supply and demand for the currency rebalance vs others. Trade adjusts, but capital flows usually decide — whether the central bank makes holding the currency desirable again, and whether spending/imports have fallen enough.¹

During defense, people think holding the level is best; Dalio stresses the opposite: an FX that helps trade and can deliver positive total return at a tolerable rate is often a lower level. A sharp quick devaluation hurts prior longs but lets new longs expect positive returns at lower real rates — exactly what weak domestic conditions need.²

At the bottom: import collapse improves the CA ~8pp of GDP; inflows stabilize; flight eases; IMF-style support is common; shorts ease after ~a year, longs stay high, shorts near pre-crisis in ~two years; inflation needs ~two years after stabilization. Imports may shrink ~10%; equities −50%+; CA from ~−6% toward ~+2% surplus (~18 months). Exports may contract in the worst phase, then rebound.³

Devaluation is like cocaine: short stimulation, ruinous if abused — watch whether printing and sliding continue.

Well managed: surprise large enough devaluation, tight money aligning demand with income, rates that compensate risk, reserves used to smooth not to pretend, orderly bad-debt work-outs. Poorly managed: expected drip devaluations, too-easy money, controls instead of adjustment, burning reserves to sustain spending, disorderly defaults. Incomes/spending revive in 1–2 years; real FX often ~10% cheap on PPP and stays cheap; strong inflows may take 4–5 years.⁴
