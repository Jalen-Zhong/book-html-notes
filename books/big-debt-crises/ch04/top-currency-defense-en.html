The top / FX-defense arrives when the inflows, rich FX, rich assets, and fast debt growth that powered the bubble become unsustainable. The mirror cycle begins: weaker inflows and asset prices worsen fundamentals, which drives further outflows — the door to a balance-of-payments crisis and inflationary depression.¹

Triggers resemble household distress: income loss, credit tightening, cost spikes, or debts too large to service. Categories: export income drops (overvalued FX or commodity price falls); import/borrowing costs rise; foreign lending/investment slows for fundamentals, fear, or tightening at home and abroad; residents themselves want capital out.²

Classic defense: burn reserves and/or hike. Neither lasts — expected gradual devaluation lifts rates via forward discounts; a small expected move can imply intolerable rate premia; spending reserves invites more speculation. Capital controls are a last ditch and usually fail via evasion and trapped-money fear. Defense often lasts ~six months with another 10–20% reserve draw before abandonment.³

Forwards often lead spots lower: hikes prop the spot while the forward weakens relative to it — a whip effect.

Bold vows that the currency will not weaken are classic — and often prologue to letting it go.⁴
