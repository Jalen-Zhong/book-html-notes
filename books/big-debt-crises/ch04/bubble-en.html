The bubble grows inside a virtuous loop: early inflows produced returns and appreciation, which pulled in more capital. FX and/or assets get bid up and increasingly debt-financed; prices are too high for adequate returns, yet borrowing continues because prices are still rising.¹

Archetype averages: foreign inflows ~10% of GDP; reserves still rising; real FX ~15% overvalued on PPP; equities often +20% over several years into the peak. Entities get structurally long the hot currency without always naming the bet — FX liabilities with local deposits, unhedged revenues, locals borrowing the 'weaker' foreign currency.²

Imports outpace exports; the current account worsens; consumption is financed by foreign capital. Productive projects thin while money chasing them thickens; appreciation erodes competitiveness. Debt/GDP rises ~10pp a year for years; FX debt often ~35% of total debt and ~45% of GDP; GDP gap ~+4%. Debt climbs from ~125% to ~150% of GDP; CA worsens ~2pp of GDP.³

The income–spending gap is patched by inflows priced for perpetual boom — a minor shock can reverse it.

Higher external dependence and bigger asset bubbles map to nastier outcomes — the grouping lesson across the 27 cases.⁴
