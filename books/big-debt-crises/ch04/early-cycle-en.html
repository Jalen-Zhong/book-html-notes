In the healthy upswing, inflows follow fundamentals: competitiveness, investable projects, clean balance sheets. Debt and incomes rise together; exports and foreign capital reinforce each other; private sector, government, and banks lever up — still sensible while incomes rise.¹

If the currency is cheap enough for FDI aimed at export markets, or the country runs net exports, the balance of payments turns favorable. The central bank's job eases: more growth per unit of inflation — via appreciation, lower rates, or reserve buildup. Many EM banks sell their own currency to buy FX and park it as reserves — savings that cushion imbalances and expand domestic liquidity.²

Early total returns look good: either the FX rises, or money creation lifts local-currency asset prices.

Capital flows are the flows to watch — most volatile. A favorable capital account lifts markets and invites still more capital, seeding the bubble act.³
