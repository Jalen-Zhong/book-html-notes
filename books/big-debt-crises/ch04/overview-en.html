§4 is the archetypal inflationary debt crisis and currency crisis. Dalio averages roughly 27 inflationary deleveragings heavy in foreign-currency debt, and points readers to the Weimar case in Part 2. The path mirrors the deflationary template until the depression stage, then forks: outflows, failed FX defense, and letting the currency go turn the bust inflationary.¹

Hard reminders: money and debt are both mediums of exchange and stores of wealth; debt is someone's asset; debts promise pay in a named currency; central banks can only print what they control; bigger piles make the wire-walk between deflationary and inflationary depression harder. Printing eases debt stress but cheapens the currency — if depreciation outruns interest compensation, holders flee and a dangerous currency dynamic begins.²

Deflationary path: selling and defaults lead. Inflationary path: FX and capital flight lead.

Who is most vulnerable? Non-reserve currencies, thin reserves, large foreign debt, widening fiscal/current-account deficits, negative real rates, and a history of inflation betrayal. Reserve-currency countries are not immune: repeated overstimulation can turn a deflationary deleveraging inflationary and erode reserve status itself.³

Sub-chapters follow five stages — early → bubble → top & FX defense → depression (let FX go) → normalization — plus a hyperinflation-spiral sidecar. Expand §4 on the home index to enter each.⁴
