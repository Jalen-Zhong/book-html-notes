§8 is the flagship deflationary deleveraging: 1920s tech boom and leveraged bubble → tightening pops it → equity crash → bank failures and protectionism rewrite recession as depression → Roosevelt’s gold break, bank holiday, and stimulus bend toward a “beautiful deleveraging” → premature tightening and the 1937–38 relapse. Same language as 2008; different speed and toolkit.¹

Reserve-currency status biased the U.S. toward a deflationary path (local-currency debts; the world still wanted dollars), yet gold discipline tied printing and devaluation at the worst moments. Read against the §3 template and §9: same cycle, learning curve sets depression length.²

“Cheap” stocks rebound while earnings have not yet collapsed—if debts and banks are still breaking, the bounce is a trap chapter.

Subs: bubble → top/crash → depression → New Deal adjustment → tightening aftermath. The book’s newsfeed of headlines and Fed bulletins is here collapsed into causal story.³
