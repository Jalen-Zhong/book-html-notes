1. Bubble boom (c. 1927–1929)

Big Debt Crises §8 · sub-chapter · story rewrite

After the war and the 1920–21 recession, the U.S. saw technology-led expansion: electrification deepening, radios going mass-market, auto ownership surging, factory output per hour rising sharply. Optimism had fundamentals; the bubble stacked leverage and narrative on top.¹

Dalio’s bubble checklist lights up: high prices, discounting still-faster gains, one-sided sentiment, leveraged buying, new buyers, stimulative policy inflating and tight policy later popping. Margin debt exploded with equities; short-rate policy became fuse management.²

The productivity story was real; buying eternity with margin is the bubble.

The earlier cycle (roughly 1922–27) looked healthier; near the top, credit tilted to speculation and maturity/risk mismatches deepened. Policy dilemma: cool speculation without wrecking the real economy—the next act writes that dilemma as rate hikes.³
