5. Tightening aftermath (1936–1938)

Big Debt Crises §8 · sub-chapter · story rewrite

Story rewrite of a case study — not verbatim. Full text: Ray Dalio, Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises (Bridgewater, 2018).

After a stretch of recovery, inflation fears and “normalization” urges returned. Higher reserve requirements drained excess reserves; fiscal policy also consolidated. Dalio stresses: the first tightening may not immediately hurt stocks and the economy, but before deleveraging is done, premature stacked tightening can send recovery back into recession—as in 1937–38.¹

Policy eased again later, but scars and politics were already written. Globally the path bent toward war economy: tariff wars, military industry, and expansionism rewrote the next cycle’s constraints—echoing §5.²

Beautiful deleveraging’s rival is not only deflation—it is victory-lap tightening too early.

§8 close: reserve-currency countries can still depression; gold and slow politics lengthen the ugly phase; gold-break plus guarantees/restructuring open a beautiful window; premature normalization relapses. §9 shows a faster-learning modern version.³
