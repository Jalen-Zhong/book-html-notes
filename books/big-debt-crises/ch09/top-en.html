2. The top (2007)

H1 2007: equities at highs, jobs and retail still strong, sentiment upbeat—while housing and the most aggressive subprime models turned non-viable. Regulators already noted that after the refi/real-estate boom, many small subprime originators’ models failed. Vol still low; cracks in the “corners.”¹

Summer liquidity froze: structured credit and interbank dollars became dear or unavailable. The public story shifted from “subprime contained” toward “this might be the big one.” Autumn brought loss prints, conduit/SIV rescue debates, and a re-learning of how risk crawled back to the core via securitization and guarantee chains.²

A top is not one day—it is a season where the whole still looks fine while credit corners are already broken.

Shared puzzle for investors and policymakers: acting enough before the real economy breaks looks “excessive”; waiting for confirming data often means the depression act has begun.³
