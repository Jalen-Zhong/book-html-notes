3. Crash & depression (2008)

Big Debt Crises §9 · sub-chapter · story rewrite

Early 2008: manufacturing, retail, and jobs softened; huge write-downs and monoline downgrades showed mark-to-market pushing losses into capital fast. March’s Bear rescue bought a brief “aftermath rally”; summer stagflation talk (oil) and the GSE conservatorships showed housing credit had become a sovereign-scale problem.¹

September’s Lehman failure pushed true depression risk on-screen: counterparties froze, money funds broke, CP seized. Policy overnight-puzzled guarantees, capital, swap lines, and a fiscal bailout fund. Dalio’s near-daily narrative stresses: the order and messaging of systemic resolutions themselves change how fast panic fuel burns.²

Letting a “too big” fail tells markets the next domino has no official floor.

Ugly-deleveraging traits complete: deleveraging, runs, fire sales, credit spreads exploding; near-zero rates make MP1 insufficient—guarantees, QE, and fiscal must arrive. Versus the 1930s, tools hit the table faster—yet the path still ran ugly-then-beautiful.³
