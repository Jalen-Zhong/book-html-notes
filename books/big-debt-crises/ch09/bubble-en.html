1. Emerging bubble (2004–2006)

Healthy early phase: debt roughly tracks income-producing activity. 1990s U.S. debt/GDP rose modestly with strong income; after the 2001 recession, ease, tech-bust cleanup, and housing-finance “innovation” pushed leverage into homes and structured products. Prices rich vs rents/incomes yet still extrapolating—bubble checklist on again.¹

Not only the U.S.: multi-country house prices and private credit co-moved. Globalization of dollar lending pushed leverage onto chains with thinner regulatory umbrellas. Dealer leverage, off-balance conduits, rating arbitrage, and derivatives were new forms of old leverage.²

When repayment depends on house-price refinancing rather than income, the debt-cycle clock is already running.

On the path from stimulus toward gradual tightening, fragility often accumulates while things “still look fine.” The top’s tell is next: real economy and stocks still strong while housing and the most aggressive lenders crack first.³
