4. Beautiful deleveraging (2009)

Big Debt Crises §9 · sub-chapter · story rewrite

Story rewrite of a case study — not verbatim. Full text: Ray Dalio, Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises (Bridgewater, 2018).

Early 2009 data were still in freefall; several giant firms remained at risk as a new administration took the reins. The turn was a coordinated monetary–fiscal–regulatory counterattack: QE and credit facilities, fiscal stimulus, bank stress tests and recapitalization. Once markets believed a systemic floor existed, risk assets and credit spreads healed first.¹

Stress tests mattered as politics as much as technique: publish gaps and ready public capital, turning unknown unknowns into a priced capital plan. The beautiful-deleveraging signature returned: nominal conditions improving, relatively fast financial recapitalization, emergency credit programs becoming exit-able.²

Ugly to beautiful is not mood magic—it is the four levers shifting from default/deflation-led to monetization-plus-restructuring balance.

Public anger and fairness stayed binding constraints; policy had to explain choices between moral hazard and systemic collapse. H2 consolidated the “beginning of beautiful deleveraging”: rule reform started, healing remained uneven.³
