5. Aftermath & lessons

Big Debt Crises §7 · sub-chapter · story rewrite

Monetary stability did not heal political trauma. Hyperinflation redistributed on a vast scale: savers and creditors were wiped; debtors and real-asset holders gained relatively; middle-class trust in “paper promises” cracked and extreme narratives found soil. Dalio notes the case set the stage for Germany’s 1930s political-economic upheaval.¹

Template lessons, still as story: with FX debt and forced outflows, deflationary “print to save” becomes an FX crisis first; after reserve defense fails, letting the currency go without closing the gap slides toward hyperinflation; exit needs relief + new money + fiscal-monetary reset—miss one and stability is fragile.²

Same debt-cycle language—Weimar writes an inflation ending, the U.S. 1929 a deflation ending—the fork is money and external debt structure.

Investor/policy memo: watch whether gaps are financed by printing-plus-flight; separate transitional inflation crises from money death; reform windows often open when social pain and creditor fear peak together. §8 applies the same language to a reserve-currency deflationary mega-case.³
