Eventually the system returns toward “normal,” but even in a beautiful deleveraging, recovery in activity and capital formation is slow. Real activity often needs roughly 5–10 years to regain prior peaks (hence “lost decade”); equities often take about a decade to reclaim highs — investors stay wary, equity risk premiums stay high.¹

Normalization is not a fairy-tale ending: leverage culture, regulatory memory, and political fractures become initial conditions for the next short cycles. After these seven acts, use the template on the book’s U.S. 2007–2011 and 1928–1937 deep dives and the ~21 deflationary chart cases — “another one of those” is a skill trained by repetition.²

Normalization means the machine turns again — not that scars vanish, nor that the next bubble is cancelled.
