§11 · GLOSSARY · EN

This glossary is not a dictionary dump—it is the recurring cast of Part 3 (and the whole book). Each term is a story-friendly shorthand so chart decks and crisis narratives snap into focus.1

Remember three pairs first: bubble/depression, ease/tighten, domestic debt/FX debt—most other terms are parts of those pairs.

balance of payments / 国际收支 — The balance of goods, services, financial assets, and other payments between residents of a country/currency and the rest of the world. Worsening feels like a household’s inflows falling short of spending; improving is the reverse.

balance-of-payments crisis / 国际收支危机 — A crisis where the BOP worsens so entities lack adequate world-market buying power—essentially out of cash and credit.

bubble / 泡沫 — A debt-cycle stage of self-reinforcing, unsustainable rises in debt, asset prices, and growth. Key word: unsustainable—temporary boom, then bust.

capital inflows/outflows / 资本流入流出 — Cross-border money/credit to buy investment assets. Foreigners buying domestic assets = inflows; domestics buying foreign assets = outflows.

currency peg / 货币钉住 — Exchange-rate policy keeping the currency fixed to another currency, a basket, or an asset such as gold.

current account / 经常账户 — Exports minus imports plus net income—roughly net income. A deficit must be financed via capital-account transactions (borrowing or equity sales).

deleveraging / 去杠杆 — Reducing debt burdens. Attribution decomposes income, inflation, paydowns, new borrowing, and interest rolled into debt.

depression / 萧条 — Severe downturn with self-reinforcing asset and growth declines—classically when central banks are constrained in easing.

easing / stimulation / 宽松与刺激 — Making money/credit more available via lower rates, printing, regulation changes, or fiscal moves. Tightening is the opposite.

FX debt / 外币债 — Debt denominated in a currency other than the investor’s home currency—the FX-crisis magnifier.

GDP / GDP gap / 产出与缺口 — GDP: value of final goods and services. GDP gap: imprecise slack vs potential—positive = little slack; negative = spare capacity.

liquidity / 流动性 — Whether money/credit are scarce or plentiful. When tight, even strong borrowers pay more.

M0 / money 0 — Base money printed: currency in circulation plus reserves at the central bank.

real / real FX / real rates — “Real” strips inflation. Real FX gauges cheap/expensive; negative real rates mean lenders lose buying power.

reflation / 再通胀 — Easy/stimulative policy helping recovery—the monetary side of beautiful deleveraging.

reserves / 外汇储备 — Government foreign-currency and/or gold savings—used to pay and to affect own-currency supply, demand, and price.

short/long rate · yield curve — Short rates ≤~3 months; long rates often 10y sovereign yields. Inverted curve (short > long) prices short rates to fall.

Official definitions and chart conventions live in the book; this page is deliberately short for navigation.2
