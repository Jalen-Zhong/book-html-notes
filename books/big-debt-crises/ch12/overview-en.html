§12 sub-chapters
  • Overview: why macroprudential
  • ① Toolkit: demand & supply

Ordinary monetary policy tries to be even-handed: ease or tighten money/credit through banks without naming who gets loans. Macroprudential does the opposite—regulatory levers steer credit away from frothy corners or toward starved ones. Higher down payments in housing bubbles, easier mortgage standards in busts: the plain picture.1

It can look fiscal (winners and losers), yet shines in deleveragings: QE may juice asset froth while the real economy still convalesces—Depression and WWII central banks used macroprudential to cool pockets while keeping overall policy easy.2

Typical use cases

  • Rate tool weak or undesirable: need stimulus at ZLB; or need local tightening without hiking the whole economy
  • Steer credit from froth toward gaps
  • Combine tools; coordinate Fed, Congress, executive, regulators
  • Success = experiment and kill failures fast; failures often leave distortive rules too long (e.g. Regulation Q)

Questions policymakers keep asking

Where are bubbles—and how sure are you? Which sectors starve—do you need differentiated credit? Political cost of picking winners? Hit demand, supply, or both? How to measure success? Second-/third-order workarounds (shadow banking)? How to empower and coordinate—UK FPC vs US FSOC are modern answers.3

The policy rate is the main valve; macroprudential is the zone valves—when the main valve can’t move, zones decide who floods and who droughts.

Early-1990s to mid-2000s under-use was the historical exception; recently tools are again seen as core. The Fed changed equity margin requirements 23 times historically—same logic, old playbook.4
