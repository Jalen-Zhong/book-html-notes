US history compressed by tool family—details from public surveys (Elliott–Feldberg–Lehnert et al.); here only when/who/what next. Full chronology is in the book appendix.1

Margin requirements

After 1934 the Fed could set broker (Reg T) and bank (Reg U) margins—up in bulls, down in bears. After 1974, derivatives and other leverage largely sidelined the tool—new pipes around the valve.2

Underwriting & mortgage standards

Depression: FHLB system and FHA insurance eased LTV/terms to restart housing credit. WWII: Roosevelt ordered Fed to tighten durables installment credit (Reg W) toward defense. 1950s: Reg X and FHA/VA down-payment/maturity tweaks cooled housing froth while the recovery was still young.3

Supply restraints & guidance

Voluntary credit restraint (Korea, 1960s BOP, 1980 Carter program), countercyclical reserve tweaks, Reg Q deposit ceilings, and supervisory “direct pressure”—from 1929 anti-speculation statements to 2000s subprime/leveraged-loan guidance—fill the supply scroll. Some worked briefly; some drove deposits to shadow banks; some bundled with rate wars on inflation.4

History doesn’t prove a perfect valve exists—only that when you rely on the policy rate alone, froth and starvation complain together, so zone tools keep returning.

Return to §3/§9 after this appendix: beautiful deleveraging is not only a printing curve—it is also who may borrow, on what terms. Macroprudential is the underestimated chapter in the debt-cycle playbook.5
