Macroprudential tools split roughly into changing borrower demand or bank supply. Practice usually combines both and swaps weapons as finance innovates—derivatives and shadow banks leak old valves.1

Demand side: make borrowing harder or easier

  • LTV and maturity caps
  • Debt-service-to-income ratios
  • Margin requirements for financial assets
  • Subsidies, tax, and rules that change loan cost
  • Down-payment/term caps on installment and mortgage credit (e.g. wartime Reg W, postwar Reg X)

Supply side: make banks more or less willing to lend

  • Capital/reserve requirements on certain lending
  • Portfolio constraints
  • Accounting rule shifts
  • Supervisory “direct pressure” / moral suasion
  • Interest/lending ceilings (Reg Q etc.)
  • Voluntary credit restraint and sector quotas (US and Europe)
Demand tools raise the gate; supply tools pinch the reservoir—shadow banking digs a new canal.

Measurement and exit matter: keep or expand what works; kill distortions within months. Painful lesson: innovation that routes around rules can hit the letter of policy while migrating risk off-umbrella.2
