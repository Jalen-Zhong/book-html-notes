Domestic basket script: debt mostly in local currency, mostly held at home; at the limit the central bank can still cut, print, buy assets, guarantee, and fiscal-stimulate to bend an ugly deleveraging into a beautiful one. US 1926–36, UK 1927–36, Japan/Nordics 1990s, and many 2008 cases are variations on that skeleton.1

Bubble: self-reinforcing debt, assets, growth

Usual tells: debt/GDP up, flashy asset returns, positive GDP gap, mild late-cycle tightening. Exceptions exist—1920s UK lacked a broad domestic bubble yet sat next to US equities and gold-standard contagion; the debt stock alone made it fragile.2

Depression: self-reinforcing ugly deleveraging

A shock (crash, bank run, cross-border ripple) hits high leverage: GDP, equities, homes, jobs fall together; debt/GDP rises via collapsing nominal incomes, deflation, and interest rolled into new debt. Bubble/depression and tight/easy gauges flip from positive to negative.3

Reflation: can policy bend ugly into beautiful?

Successful cases share: break gold peg or equivalent constraint, lift nominal growth above nominal rates, manage banks and bad debts aggressively (more of the classic 9 levers → usually faster). Debt-burden falls mainly via real incomes, then mild inflation or paydowns. Politics often shift—FDR after the Depression is the textbook.4

Domestic-currency privilege: you can print your own money. The cost of waiting: deflation eats a generation’s balance sheets—and ballots—first.
