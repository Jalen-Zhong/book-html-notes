FX-basket core fragility: liabilities booked in someone else’s money. Inflows fuel bubbles while real FX often richens; when inflows stop, FX defense, hikes, reserve burn, and depreciation hit together—and local-currency burden of FX debt jumps. Germany 1918, LatAm 1980s, Asia 1997, repeated Russia episodes, Iceland 2008 tell one story in different accents.1

Bubble: FX leverage + eroded competitiveness

Tells: high FX debt share, current-account dependence, strong real FX, juicy asset returns, reversible inflows. War cases (Germany) may skip a classic bubble and plant the bomb via wartime finance and reparations.2

Bust / currency crisis: sudden stop

Shocks: foreign rates, commodities, neighbor contagion, politics. Inflows collapse; reserves defend; short rates spike; real FX plunges; GDP and equities fall; inflation rises with the currency. Debt/GDP rises from income collapse and from FX revaluing FX debt.3

Policy fork: let tightening bite, or print the hole

Key: allow financial conditions to correct the external gap, or print to replace outflows—the latter risks FX–inflation spirals. More classic risk factors (fiscal holes, too-low real rates, thin reserves, weak credibility) → higher hyperinflation odds. Stops usually need a currency reset, fiscal anchor, external help (e.g. IMF), and structural change—not “print a bit more.”4

In an FX debt crisis the printing press cannot outrun foreigners’ ATMs—unless you first rebuild an anchor others will hold.
