Part 3 is not 48 country biographies. It runs the same debt-cycle machine across currencies and eras so you see: bubble–depression–reflation keeps recurring; what changes is domestic vs foreign debt, reserve-currency status, and how fast/bravely policy levers are pulled.1

Two baskets: ①primarily domestic-currency debt (typically deflationary deleveragings, ~21 cases); ②non-domestic / FX debt (typically inflationary deleveragings and BOP crises, ~27 cases). Blank charts mean missing data; some post-WWII Germany/Italy/Japan samples were excluded for data gaps.2

Charts rhyme the same sentence: debt burdens fall via rising incomes, inflation, default/restructuring, paydowns—or via printing and FX revaluation. Mixes differ; stories differ.

Read this overview for the fork between the two crisis types, then the domestic and foreign pattern pages. Full chart decks live in the book; this is a navigable patterns guide, not 48 duplicated decks.3

  • Domestic basket (21): United States 1929 · UK 1929 · Japan 1929 · France 1929 · UK 1943 · United States 1945 · Norway 1990 · Finland 1991 …
  • Foreign basket (27): Germany 1918 · Argentina 1980 · Brazil 1980 · Chile 1981 · Mexico 1981 · Peru 1982 · Philippines 1983 · Malaysia 1984 …
