          A securitization warehouse is the interim account where an arranger warehouses collateral on short-term funding before a CDO/ABS prints. Warehouses boost fees and turnover — and leave pipeline risk on or near the balance sheet if the issuance window shuts. Crisis histories treat warehouses and unsold tranches as key transmission points in structured credit.
          <div class="why">Why it matters: turns “Walmart turnover” from a retail metaphor into a checkable pipe.</div>
          <div class="src">Source: FCIC and structured-credit primers on CDO pipelines/warehouses</div>

      <h2 id="s7">Wall Street mode: leverage toward 20% ROE</h2>
      <p>The board wants hedge-fund-like ~20% ROE. Pig’s third play: “Wall Street mode” — gear up. His arithmetic is blunt: ~5% asset yield, ~3% funding cost, ~20× leverage, and the gross return jumps into a board-friendly zone; after pay, shareholder ROE can still clear 20%. Firms such as Merrill and Bear were widely discussed pre-crisis at 30×-plus leverage (depending on accounting perimeter)<a class="fn-link" href="#fn7"><sup class="fn">⁷</sup></a>.</p>
      <p>Prop desks boom. An IB veteran sighs: the firm is becoming a hedge fund — leveraging shareholder capital for spread, not living only on client franchise.</p>
