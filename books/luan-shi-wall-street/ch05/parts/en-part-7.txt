      <h2 id="s3">Allegory: Hyper Goldman’s board</h2>
      <p>To make ROE human, the author stages a Wall Street animal fable: “Hyper Goldman Securities” (超盛). CEO Ox runs the firm; advisor Pig claims to be great at math — and mostly means arithmetic. Around them: the IB lead, capital markets, a securitization star, a prop-investing chief, and traders who, once big enough, leave for hedge funds.</p>
      <p>The board does not grade Ox on storytelling. The ruler is ROE. Pig therefore walks to the blackboard.</p>

      <div class="scene">
        Hyper Goldman is not a courtroom transcript of one real bank; it is theater — Pig’s arithmetic for laughs, the board’s ROE obsession for the sting.

      <h2 id="s4">DuPont on the blackboard</h2>
      <p>Pig writes the DuPont breakdown: take “are we earning enough on equity?” and factor it into three manageable multiplications — profit margin × asset turnover × financial leverage<a class="fn-link" href="#fn3"><sup class="fn">³</sup></a><a class="fn-link" href="#fn4"><sup class="fn">⁴</sup></a>. Raise any factor, and ROE rises; the industry’s arms race is really a race over which factor still has room.</p>
