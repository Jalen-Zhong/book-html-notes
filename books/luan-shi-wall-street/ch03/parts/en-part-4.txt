        <div class="fn-head">Section notes</div>
        <div class="fn-item" id="fn8">
          <strong>⁸ Goldman’s retention culture</strong>
          Elite banks retain high-potential interns and analysts with internal rotations, cross-group intros, and partner time — turning “exit” into “you haven’t finished the map.” Public campus narratives cast Goldman as elite filter plus internal mobility: stayers thicken the “Because we are Goldman” glue; leavers carry the halo to the next table. The grander the retention, the clearer his choice: not “failed to get Goldman,” but “refused the staff chair.”
          <div class="why">Why it matters: closes the chapter’s theme — prestige path (Frank / Lehman / Goldman retain) versus trading path (Vince / munis / I DO).</div>
          <div class="src">Sources: Goldman public culture and recruiting narratives; read against Chapter 2’s internship bonus/culture frame</div>
        </div>
        <div class="fn-item" id="fn9">
          <strong>⁹ Munis inside the rates org chart</strong>
          On bank FICC trees, muni desks often sit near rates or public-finance lines: sharing curves, repo, and hedges while keeping tax-and-credit specialty. An offer for “muni trading in rates” means daily language is rates, dialect is muni — exactly why Roger needed a curve-model translator.
          <div class="why">Why it matters: places “munis” from a niche label back into a concrete ward of the rates empire.</div>
          <div class="src">Sources: public FICC business overviews; rates–muni crossover primers</div>
