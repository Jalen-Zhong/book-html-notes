      <h2 id="s1">Classmate shock: why a commercial bank</h2>
      <p>Berkeley classmate Frank nearly froze when he heard the author was interviewing at a commercial bank: you already had Goldman on the résumé — why step onto the “deposit-and-loan” track that sounded less glamorous than a pure investment bank<a class="fn-link" href="#fn1"><sup class="fn">¹</sup></a>? Frank’s own next stop was Lehman Brothers — still, in 2005–06, a top-table name on Wall Street league lists, especially loud in fixed-income research and trading, the sort of brand that “sounded right” to peers<a class="fn-link" href="#fn2"><sup class="fn">²</sup></a>.</p>
      <p>The author kept a different ruler. He did not want another tour as a strategy staff officer; he wanted a trading seat. He would rather trade pork-belly futures first — a contract that sounded like a wet market yet ticked with real P&amp;L — than keep polishing client slides<a class="fn-link" href="#fn3"><sup class="fn">³</sup></a>. The IB business card was fragrant; the pilot’s chair kept him awake.</p>
