      <h2 id="s4">Vince enters: munis and a church organ</h2>
      <p>By then despair was near: the free-food interviewers had been polite and distant, confirming “you’re not our people.” Then Vince walked in — head of the municipal desk. He took no free food. Small talk surfaced a hobby: church organ. The room cut from salad-and-apple power games to a human corner.</p>
      <p>Municipal bonds finance states, cities, school districts, and public agencies, often with federal tax-exempt interest, fragmented issuers, fiddly credit and tax clauses, and liquidity nothing like Treasuries<a class="fn-link" href="#fn6"><sup class="fn">⁶</sup></a>. In the mid-2000s fashion parade of subprime, CDS, and exotics, a muni seat did not sound trendy — yet it sat at a real junction of rates and credit.</p>

      <h2 id="s5">Trader qualities: risk, discipline, observation</h2>
      <p>Vince asked what makes a good trader. The author skipped “I love markets” porridge and answered three battlefield rules: control risk — models are not enough; there is still exposure, liquidity, and human error; self-discipline under fire — execute the rules when the tape humiliates you, instead of revenge-sizing; observe and adapt — curves and order flow change; dogma dies first.</p>
      <div class="scene">
        Those three demote “can calculate” to a ticket and promote “leave the table alive” to the main course — exactly a desk head’s palate.
      </div>

      <h2 id="s6">I DO: the muni seat and Roger</h2>
      <p>Vince offered a municipal trading seat on the spot. Hesitation flickered: munis were less trendy than subprime, CDS, or exotics. It lasted one beat — then he heard himself say I DO. He wanted the seat, not the magazine cover.</p>
      <p>On the desk was Roger: senior trader, Chicago Booth, ex-Goldman muni prop. He partnered with Vince and needed someone who could talk to the quants — rates-curve models and relative-value frames were the financial-engineering interface the author could actually plug into<a class="fn-link" href="#fn7"><sup class="fn">⁷</sup></a>. Not a pure yell-and-fill seat; a middleware job between trading gut and model shop.</p>
