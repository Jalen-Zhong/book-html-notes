      <p>To explain why only the Fed can “add water,” the author rewinds to the Panic of 1907: trust-company runs, a broken clearing chain, and J.P. Morgan locking Wall Street’s chiefs inside his library to improvise a temporary central bank with private capital and personal authority.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn1"><sup class="fn">¹</sup></a></p>
      <p>That library rescue fed the Federal Reserve Act of 1913: a public–private hybrid—a bankers’ bank with what we now call a dual mandate. It is not an ordinary commercial bank, yet it owns the system’s liquidity backstop.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn2"><sup class="fn">²</sup></a></p>
