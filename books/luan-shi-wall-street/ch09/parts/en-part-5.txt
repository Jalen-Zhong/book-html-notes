      <p>Strip Chapter 8’s indexes and acronyms, and the summer storm was a modern bank run: not depositors in a lobby queue, but cash yanking simultaneously out of repo, commercial paper, and shadow-bank conduits. Private balance sheets pledged and haircut each other; once collateral lost trust, liquidity ebbed like a tide.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn3"><sup class="fn">³</sup></a></p>
      <p>This chapter states the blunt rule: only a central bank—the Federal Reserve—can truly inject system-wide liquidity. Strong private hands can catch assets and shout “buying opportunity,” but refilling the pipes still depends on the discount window and open-market operations.</p>
