      <h2 id="s4">Strong hands: Citadel, BofA, the bounce</h2>
      <p>Strong hands showed up. Citadel took nearly all of Sowood’s book after July’s credit smash—capital and nerve buying risk at fire-sale marks.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn5"><sup class="fn">⁵</sup></a> Bank of America injected about $2 billion of convertible preferred into Countrywide, a balance-sheet sedative for the nation’s largest mortgage lender.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn4"><sup class="fn">⁴</sup></a></p>
      <p>ABX and related subprime gauges bounced; municipal basis tightened from roughly 140 toward 110—not a cured patient, just the sharpest panic blade pulled back. A generation of traders revived the mantra that every crisis is a buying opportunity—S&amp;L, Russia, the dot-com bust—each corpse had minted fortunes in the rubble. This time, they thought the script would rhyme again.</p>
