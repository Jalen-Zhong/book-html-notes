        <div class="fn-item" id="fn6">
          <strong>⁶ Auction-rate securities (ARS) and Dutch auctions</strong>
          ARS are typically long municipal bonds, corporates, or preferreds whose rates reset in periodic Dutch auctions: buyers bid the lowest rate they will accept; the clearing rate becomes the reset for holders and ownership turns over. Broker-dealers historically backstopped auctions with proprietary bids; from mid-February 2008 major firms withdrew support, auctions failed widely, holders could not exit as expected, and some municipal issuers faced penalty rates. Later buyback settlements with regulators followed.
          <div class="why">Why it matters: ARS is the retail version of “long asset pretending to be short cash”—same family as repo and ABCP.</div>
          <div class="src">Sources: CRS / Chicago Fed / Wikipedia summaries of the Feb 2008 ARS freeze; SEC and state AG settlement coverage</div>
