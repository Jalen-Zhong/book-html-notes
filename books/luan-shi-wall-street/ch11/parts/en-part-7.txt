          <strong>² Money-market funds</strong>
          Open-end funds that invest mainly in high-quality, short-duration instruments (Treasuries, commercial paper, repo, etc.), aiming for stable NAV, cash-like liquidity, and a yield edge over deposits. In 2007–08 some funds strained on ABCP and related paper; the September 2008 Reserve Primary Fund “break the buck” episode later became a confidence landmark (slightly after this chapter’s core window, same mechanism).
          <div class="why">Why it matters: they are the pipes that look like cash—when the pipes clog, wholesale funding loses oxygen.</div>

        <div class="fn-item" id="fn3">
          <strong>³ Repo haircuts</strong>
          In a repurchase agreement, the cash lender typically advances less than the collateral’s face or market value; the percentage gap is the haircut. Higher haircuts mean less cash against the same bonds—forced de-leveraging. In the crisis, on-the-run Treasuries often kept easier terms while housing-related collateral saw haircuts explode or bids refused—the same “bloodstream” sorted by collateral quality and expelled the weak.
          <div class="why">Why it matters: haircuts are the invisible leverage dial—tightening them kills positions faster than a downgrade.</div>
