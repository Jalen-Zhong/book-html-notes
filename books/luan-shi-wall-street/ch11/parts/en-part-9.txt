      <h2 id="s4">Early 2008: differentiated haircuts and Kerviel</h2>
      <p>By early 2008, haircuts sorted by pedigree: on-the-run Treasuries still financed relatively easily; MBS worsened; hedge-fund “elephants” might still negotiate ~20% haircuts, while the shrimp were simply refused.</p>
      <p>In January, European equities fell on the order of ~6% in a day and S&amp;P futures flirted with limit-down atmosphere; the Fed answered with an <strong>emergency cut</strong>—the book’s narrative often compresses this to ~50 bp, while the public record shows a <strong>75</strong> bp intermeeting cut on January 22, then another <strong>50</strong> bp at the month-end meeting.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn7"><sup class="fn">⁷</sup></a></p>
      <p>Then came Jérôme Kerviel and Société Générale: unauthorized European index-futures positions on the order of ~€50 billion notional, a net loss near €4.9 billion after unwind.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn5"><sup class="fn">⁵</sup></a> The author notes that the Fed was briefly fooled by the surface story of a “macro crash”—demons appear when liquidity ebbs.</p>
