      <p>Underneath sat internal hedging: move risk between desks inside the firm first, keep P&amp;L on the house books when possible, instead of paying the street for every hedge and advertising intent. For a rookie that was organizational lesson one — trading is outbound bets and inbound coordination.</p>

      <div class="scene">
        Done! is an acoustic period; internal hedges are the accounting period — together they define when a trade is truly finished.

      <h2 id="s5">FICC empire: Treasuries to swaps</h2>
      <p>Sales-and-trading’s big non-equity bucket is often called FICC: fixed income, currencies, commodities — beside equities, FX, and commodities lines. At many banks, fixed income was frequently the loudest P&amp;L engine, not because it was glamorous, but because balance-sheet and rate risk are huge by nature<a class="fn-link" href="#fn5"><sup class="fn">⁵</sup></a>.</p>
      <p>Inside rates/credit: Treasuries; agencies (including Fannie/Freddie-linked credit); MBS; munis; corporates (IG vs junk); and swaps/derivatives. Derivatives are not “only gambling” — firms use them to lock costs, reshape duration, hedge inventory; the same tools can also lever directional bets. He reminded himself to hear swaps as risk language first; in Chinese public talk that flattens complex derivatives into pure “gambling,” be careful not to erase legitimate hedges<a class="fn-link" href="#fn6"><sup class="fn">⁶</sup></a>.</p>

      <h2 id="s6">What munis are: tax exemption &amp; federalism</h2>
      <p>Municipal bonds fund states, localities, and public-related issuers — utilities, universities, hospitals, toll authorities. Qualifying interest is often federally tax-exempt, and sometimes state-tax-exempt too for residents — for high-bracket buyers the combined relief can reach eye-watering effective levels (public discussion often cites stacked marginal rates in the ~40%+ neighborhood)<a class="fn-link" href="#fn7"><sup class="fn">⁷</sup></a>.</p>
      <p>Buyers are therefore tax-sensitive: wealthy individuals, tax-aware corporates, specialized accounts. Deeper still is U.S. federalism: states and cities finance capital projects in the market; the federal government subsidizes via tax expenditure rather than writing every check from Washington. The muni market is federalism written onto a balance sheet.</p>

      <div class="footnotes" id="fn-block-6">
        <div class="fn-head">Section notes</div>
        <div class="fn-item" id="fn7">
          <strong>⁷ Muni tax exemption and federalism</strong>
          Most qualifying muni interest is exempt from federal income tax; in-state buyers may also escape state (and local) tax — double or triple exemption. Coupons are typically lower than taxable peers, yet after-tax attractive to high brackets. Federalism angle: localities borrow for schools, roads, utilities; Congress lowers their funding cost through tax preference. In South Carolina v. Baker (1988) the Court held the federal government is not constitutionally barred from taxing state-bond interest — yet the exemption framework largely endured as a signature of U.S. public finance.
          <div class="why">Why it matters: without tax and federalism, munis look like a sleepy niche; with them, the desk’s buyers and purpose snap into focus.</div>

      <h2 id="s7">Lucy’s logic: cheap funding → prop lean</h2>
      <p>Lucy Bank had deep pockets and relatively cheap funding — especially as a foreigner-flavored commercial-bank balance sheet. Its client franchise, though, was often weaker than Goldman’s or Lehman’s: not every mandate walked in the door<a class="fn-link" href="#fn8"><sup class="fn">⁸</sup></a>.</p>
      <p>Strategy followed: less pure agency flow, more proprietary risk. The muni desk in this chapter leans prop — and the eternal tension remains: sales want clients and wallet share; trading wants inventory risk and P&amp;L. When prop logic dominates, the table is a chessboard with the firm’s own chips, not merely a commission pipe.</p>

        Cheap funding is ammo; a thin franchise is blank space on the map; a prop lean is Lucy’s fill-in-the-blank — and the politics a rookie must learn.
