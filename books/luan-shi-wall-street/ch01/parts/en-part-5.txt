eaning, from strengths to limits, sounding like he truly knew it. That round passed too.</p>
      <h2>New York HQ: a two-day marathon</h2>
      <p>The final battle was at Goldman’s New York headquarters. Fixed Income Strategies and Investment Banking Strategies would each put him through a full day with nine-plus interviewers<a class="fn-link" href="#fn11"><sup class="fn">¹¹</sup></a>. Classic bank style: skills and chemistry. Goldman especially prizes teamwork; a new hire must feel “comfortable” to work with.</p>
      <p>Day one with Fixed Income Strategies went smoothly. Beyond financial math: lots of probability. Peking University training plus casino miles made coin flips, dice, and cards feel like home forms. There were riddles too — on an island, four identical-looking pills mixed, how do you guarantee one A and one B each day? Answer: quarter every pill; eat one quarter of each daily.</p>
      <p>Day two was IB Strategies — Keen’s group. Morning flowed. At lunch Keen chatted while sizing him up, then warned kindly: morning looked good; the first afternoon interviewer was senior MD Nick — clear that hurdle and you were basically in.</p>
      <p>Nick, a professor-turned-banker with a warm smile, wandered topics, then asked: what looks like a good investment now? Open question. Equities felt too ordinary; he needed something fresher. He remembered an emerging-market bond fund he had bought and argued: EM sovereign bonds looked attractive — after the Russia crisis and Argentina’s default<a class="fn-link" href="#fn12"><sup class="fn">¹²</sup></a>, investors were still scared, prices low and yields high; yet the world had shifted, China’s rise lifting commodity prices, resource exporters’ balances improving, debt service easing; commodity investment also brought capital inflows and possible FX upside; recent RMB appreciation was another catalyst<a class="fn-link" href="#fn13"><sup class="fn">¹³</sup></a>. Buy now and you might harvest coupon, price, and currency together.</p>
      <p>Nick lit up; they debated eagerly. The author guessed Goldman wanted people who could make almost any case coherent. He thought both desks might want him; Fixed Income was closer to the trader dream, IB Strategies’ storytelling not bad either.</p>
      <h2>Ending: assigned to IB Strategies</h2>
      <p>Back in California, Debbie was glowing: Goldman Strategies had taken several Berkeley students — unprecedented — and he was wanted by both Fixed Income and IB. Then the formal note: after internal coordination, he was “assigned” to Investment Banking Strategies.</p>
      <p>Excitement mixed with a little disappointment — abandon the trader ideal to become an investment banker’s strategist? Debbie’s bright eyes held him; he knew there was no choice but to accept the assignment. The Gilded Age opening freezes on that first true step into Goldman’s front-office world.</p>
