      <p>Meanwhile government-linked issuers paired floaters with swaps until balance-sheet management looked hedge-fundized. Borrowing was no longer only for bridges and schools; it was also curve and basis. Economy-wide leverage seeped from Street desks into public issuers’ toolkits.</p>

      <hr class="divider" />

      <h2 id="s11">First ship on fire: Bear’s related funds</h2>
      <p>In June 2007, Bear Stearns–related high-grade structured-credit hedge funds hit the wall: subprime-linked holdings, leverage, and margin calls shredded the “high grade” story<a class="fn-link" href="#fn9"><sup class="fn">⁹</sup></a>. The parent pledged billions to slow a fire sale—on the interlocking fleet, one ship finally burned in public.</p>
      <p>Chapter 7’s stratagem-within-stratagem lands cold: tranching does not cancel risk; it only reorders who burns first. When housing, funding, and correlation turn together, chained ships light together.</p>
