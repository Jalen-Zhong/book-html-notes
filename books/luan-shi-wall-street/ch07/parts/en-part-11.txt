          In February 2007 HSBC issued a trading update on U.S. Mortgage Services, warning loan-impairment charges would run well above consensus, driven by deteriorating subprime and related ARM/second-lien books. Household International, bought in 2003, sat inside that U.S. consumer-finance footprint. Retrospectives often treat the alert as an early public signal that subprime had entered large-bank earnings.
          <div class="why">Why it matters: nails party-chat “subprime” to a global bank’s disclosure.</div>
