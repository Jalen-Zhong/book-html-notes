      <p>In August 1998, Russia’s sovereign debt crisis and ruble turmoil ripped open the word “diversification.”<a class="fn-link" href="#fn4"><sup class="fn">⁴</sup></a> Positions that looked uncorrelated in peacetime fell the same way in panic — correlations twisted toward one. LTCM was too large to exit cleanly: selling <em>was</em> part of the price.</p>
      <p>What followed was a private rescue coordinated among major counterparties at the New York Fed — not mercy for genius, but a firebreak against cascading liquidations. The rehearsal ends with a cruel subtitle: when everyone needs liquidity at once, model “mean reversion” can go offline.</p>

      <h2 id="s5">Models fail: people are not Brownian particles</h2>
      <p>The rehearsal closes on Newton’s almost proverbial sigh in finance: he could calculate the motions of heavenly bodies, but not the madness of people. Market “particles” imitate, panic, and rush the same exit — they are not independent Brownian noise.</p>
