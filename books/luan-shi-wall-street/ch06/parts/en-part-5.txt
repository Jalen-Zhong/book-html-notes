      <p>The author arrives on Wall Street with a Chinese sense of cycles: dynasties rise and fall, bulls and bears rhyme, history bends back to bite its own tail. Many American colleagues default to a different clock — a ray sloping up and to the right, where technology, institutions, and markets push the world forward and drawdowns are mere interludes.</p>
      <p>On the surface, 2006 is still calm: tight spreads, loud laughter, bonus stories still writing themselves. Yet he keeps smelling an older weather in the air — not one red blotch on a screen, but “chaos” returning like a tide from far offshore.</p>
