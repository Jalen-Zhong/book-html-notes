      <p>On paper the Bear Stearns structured-credit hedge funds were not “large”: net asset value sat around the $1.6 billion mark. In Street terms that should have been corner noise.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn1"><sup class="fn">¹</sup></a></p>
      <p>What scared people was leverage — assets scaled into the tens of billions — and the lenders who took the collateral. If that collateral was haircut harder or auctioned into a thin market, losses traveled along the financing chain onto desks that thought they were unrelated. In the shadow-banking building, these funds were load-bearing walls: not huge rooms, but walls whose failure redistributes the whole load.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn2"><sup class="fn">²</sup></a></p>
