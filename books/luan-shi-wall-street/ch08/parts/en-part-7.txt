      <p>It was Chapter 7’s interlocking stratagem continued: tranching renamed risk, leverage scaled the name into size, and incomplete hedges left gaps for a summer wind to find.</p>

      <div class="scene">
        The term sheet promised AAA and hedges; high summer asked how much the hedges really covered — and what the collateral fetched under an auction hammer.
      </div>

      <hr class="divider" />

      <h2 id="s3">Cayne’s injection, Merrill’s auction threat, ABX smash</h2>
      <p>CEO Jimmy Cayne reluctantly backed a roughly $1.6 billion collateralized funding backstop for the High-Grade fund — the parent putting its own balance sheet in front of a liquidation.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn1"><sup class="fn">¹</sup></a></p>
      <p>Creditors were unconvinced. Merrill Lynch threatened a public auction of about $800 million of CDO collateral: one thin-market print could become the mark-to-market window paper everyone else had to respect.<a class="fn-link" href="#fn3"><sup class="fn">³</sup></a><a class="fn-link" href="#fn4"><sup class="fn">⁴</sup></a> Panic smashed through the ABX complex; Sowood and others showed stress in the same weather; by late July the Bear funds were headed into bankruptcy.</p>
