          <strong>⁷ SIFMA / LIBOR ratio</strong>
          The SIFMA Municipal Swap Index (formerly BMA) reflects weekly reset rates on high-grade tax-exempt floating munis; traders watch its ratio to a taxable floating benchmark (then often LIBOR). Ratio arb and hedges assume a stable relationship; when risk aversion rises, tax-exempt buyers step back, or structured books deleverage, the ratio can gap and smash positions sized to a quiet historical band.
          <div class="why">Why it matters here: the coordinate axis for the author’s personal trading lesson.</div>
          <div class="src">Sources: SIFMA index public materials; muni-derivatives market practice summaries</div>

        <div class="fn-item" id="fn8">
          <strong>⁸ Municipal basis</strong>
          Here, basis is the gap between cash muni exposure and hedge instruments (swaps, ratio locks, Treasury futures, etc.). In calm markets it trades in a band; in a liquidity crisis, cash bids vanish while derivatives still mark, and basis can gap wider (in the book’s telling, roughly 80 → 120 → 140), forcing desks to choose between cutting risk and eating margin.
          <div class="why">Why it matters here: turns abstract “deleveraging” into a ruler on the muni P&amp;L.</div>
