      <h2 id="s1">Special-forces vibe: autumn in IBD Strategies</h2>
      <p>In autumn 2005 the author walked into Goldman Sachs Investment Banking Division Strategies as an intern. The culture did not feel like a normal corporation: it moved like special forces — smart, hungry, willing to carry weight. Much of that temperament traced, in firm lore, to J. Aron, the commodities-and-FX house Goldman bought in 1981, which seeded a generation of risk-tolerant traders<a class="fn-link" href="#fn1"><sup class="fn">¹</sup></a>. Lloyd Blankfein rose on that line: precious-metals salesman at J. Aron, then currencies and commodities leadership, then the top of the firm; by 2006 he would be chairman and CEO. Around 2005, the firm’s appetite for risk was being lifted by exactly that trading bloodline<a class="fn-link" href="#fn2"><sup class="fn">²</sup></a>.</p>
      <p>For someone fresh from Berkeley’s financial-engineering program and finally sitting near the front office, the culture was both intoxicating and crushing: you were not here to “sample a workplace”; you were here to prove you belonged at the table.</p>
