class="fn">⁷</sup></a>.</p>
      <p>The practical advice was pure Wall Street: for sensitive talk, prefer the phone to email — email leaves a forwardable trail. For a newcomer this was not a how-to on cutting corners; it was a warning that information boundaries here are sharper than any campus ethics seminar.</p>

      <h2 id="s7">Meeting Rick: “Lucy Bank” prop / rates</h2>
      <p>At the NYU fair he met Rick — from the book’s aliased “Lucy Bank,” on a proprietary-trading / rates-related team. They talked bonds, rates, and the CFA: the charter path as a common signal in fixed income and portfolio work<a class="fn-link" href="#fn9"><sup class="fn">⁹</sup></a>.       <p>Prop desks then were still banks’ relatively free corners for betting the house’s capital: high payouts, real autonomy, not yet remade by the later Volcker Rule<a class="fn-link" href="#fn10"><sup class="fn">¹⁰</sup></a>. To someone who had trained expected value at blackjack and poker, the seat smelled more like a true table than client narrative decks. The chapter closes on that pull — the Goldman internship opened a door; the next table might not be in the same building.</p>
