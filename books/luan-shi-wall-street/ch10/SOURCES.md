## Fact / footnote research URLs

### Citigroup / Merrill late-2007 writedowns (~$10B / ~$9.4B narrative anchors)
- Citigroup 2007 disclosures and press coverage of multi-billion subprime/CDO loss and writedown revisions (deep autumn into Q4; later Q4 pre-tax writedown totals larger still)
- Merrill Lynch Q3 2007 FICC writedowns ~$7.9B on CDOs/U.S. subprime (SEC Exhibit / earnings materials); subsequent enlargements into year-end
- Reuters and wire coverage of the late-2007 “core bank writedown wave” as crisis wave two
- Chapter figures ~$10B (Citi) and ~$9.4B (Merrill) used as story anchors for the reopen-fire moment, not as a single-line 8-K quote

### First Franklin (Merrill, Sept 2006 agreement)
- National City / Merrill Lynch press materials (5 Sep 2006): ~$1.3B for First Franklin origination franchise and related servicing; separate whole-loan purchase of First Franklin–originated mortgages
- LA Times / Reuters contemporaneous reports; HousingWire follow-ons on shuttering the First Franklin experiment
- Crisis retrospectives linking vertical integration (originate → securitize) to Merrill’s later warehouse/CDO losses

### Bank CDS spreads (~500 bps fear reading)
- Standard crisis histories of dealer/bank CDS widening in late 2007–2008
- Bloomberg and contemporaneous credit-market reporting citing multi-hundred-bp spreads on large broker-dealers (Merrill ~500 bps as book fear anchor)

### Bank holding company (BHC) status
- Federal Reserve Board approvals (21 Sep 2008) for Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley to become bank holding companies
- Fed / contemporary explainers on BHC oversight, deposit subsidiaries, and access to the discount window / payments system in crisis

### Buffett preferred capital (Goldman prototype)
- Berkshire Hathaway ~$5B preferred investment in Goldman Sachs (Sep 2008): ~10% dividend, call premium, warrants; concurrent Goldman common equity raise
- NBC / Reuters / wire contemporaneous coverage of the confidence-and-capital signaling

### “Toxic assets / toxic waste” vernacular
- 2007–08 financial media, congressional hearing language, and desk slang for illiquid subprime CDOs and structured leftovers
- Post-crisis policy documents retaining “toxic assets” as a keyword for mark, ring-fence, and buyer-of-last-resort debates

### Allegory / literary framing (not financial primary sources)
- Hyper Goldman Act II (pipeline leftovers, MTM vs fair-value tricks, 20¢ distressed sale, three endings) is book-side allegory patterned on publicly known crisis toolkits
- Cao Cao / Liu Bei / Sun Quan three-kingdoms framing is literary only
