          <strong>⁴ Bank holding company (BHC) and the Fed window</strong>
          In the U.S., BHCs sit under the Bank Holding Company Act and Fed oversight; conversion (or financial-holding status) lets firms operate deposit subsidiaries in a fuller banking frame and, in crisis, reach the discount window, payments rails, and later liquidity facilities more cleanly. In September 2008 Goldman and Morgan Stanley won approval to become BHCs—widely read as the last two large standalone investment banks trading independence for the central-bank safety net.
